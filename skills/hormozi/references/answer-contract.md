# Answer Contract

Use the smallest format that makes the choice easy to understand and check.

Read [communication-standard.md](communication-standard.md). Apply `HZ-SYN-004` to the whole answer.

A useful answer covers these points. Combine them when that makes the answer shorter.

1. **Answer** — Name the main problem in one plain sentence.
2. **Why** — Show the current facts. Say where they came from and when they may go stale.
3. **What to do** — Give one plan in priority order.
4. **What not to do yet** — Name the tempting work that will not fix the main problem.
5. **Numbers** — Show the starting numbers, guesses, formula, range, and key change when math helps.
6. **Next step** — Name the owner, action, date, measure, safety limit, and what must happen before moving on.
7. **Sources and limits** — Cite Hormozi rules and business evidence. Label your own conclusion. Keep unknowns and safety limits clear.

## Citation Forms

```text
[HZ-OFE-001 · $100M Offers, p. 80]
[HZ-DEM-004 · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PNo9aYaPXEY&t=0s · reviewed 00:00–00:51]
[Business · BUSINESS.md > Economics]
[Business authority · relative/or/absolute/path]
```

Never cite a retrieved chunk without confirming its locator and source metadata. Never present a medium-attribution video candidate as a reviewed doctrine rule.

## Clear Writing

Give the answer first. Use short words and short sentences. Use active voice. Explain hard terms in plain words. Keep exact facts, math, sources, and safety limits.

The headings must be plain too. Use **Main problem**, **Why**, **What to do**, **What not to do yet**, and **Next step** when headings help. Keep internal labels out of the answer unless the user needs the exact term.

The answer must pass the strict plain-language check before it is sent. Fix it and check it again until it passes.

Do not cosplay, use catchphrases as proof, or claim what Alex “would definitely do.” Say what the source supports, what the business facts show, and what you conclude.
