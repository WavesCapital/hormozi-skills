# Help Buyers Get Value and Stay

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Use plain words without losing the cause, cost or condition.

Read this for churn, onboarding, early value, renewal or a customer journey. Churn means buyers leaving over a set time. Help buyers want to stay while earning a profit. Low churn alone is not the goal.

## Find What Makes Buyers Stay

Ask people with strong results how they work. Look for shared practices, then test what fits this firm. The book's gym examples are not fixed rules for every business.

An activation point is an early action or result linked to a longer stay. Find good customers and study what they did and what help they got. Use that to form a testable idea. Compare similar buyers who did and did not take that step, over the same time span.

The source starts with past buyers who stayed at least three months, then studies high spenders. It calls three months a choice, not a rule. That selected group can suggest a cause; it cannot prove one. Buyers who stay longer also have more time to spend and use the product.

Count all low-use and high-use buyers, not just those who quit. More leavers from one group may just mean that group is larger. Compare each group's share who stay. Test whether help with the step improves their results and stay. Do not promise that a login, three members or level three stops churn forever.

Sources: Retention Playbook, physical pages 5–9 and 17–23. Fair group comparisons and cause checks are application limits.

## Pick the Help That Fits

| Part | What the advisor should check |
|---|---|
| Early value | Which useful first result matters here? What evidence links it to staying? |
| Onboarding | Teach the buyer how to get that result. Make the next step and contact clear. |
| Rewards | Reward real progress with useful perks the firm can afford. |
| Peer links | Connect people when shared help adds value. |
| Group conduct | Reward helpful acts. Explain and address harmful acts under the agreed rules. |
| Payment choices | Compare full service cost and lost sales, not just cash collected now. |
| Exit feedback | Learn what failed. Offer a real fix when the buyer wants help. |
| Surveys | Ask what buyers most want to keep and would least miss. Check real results too. |
| Customer journey | Plan first value, honest proof, referrals and a useful next purchase. |

The book says not every part fits every firm. It favors personal onboarding, but also shows a one-to-six group that builds peer ties. Choose from buyer results, relationships and delivery cost. One-on-one is not always best.

First value comes first in the source journey. Proof, referrals and upgrades may follow in different orders. Do not demand a review or referral before helping a buyer or selling a fitting upgrade.

Sources: pages 16–35; the group example is page 24 and flexible order is pages 30 and 35.

## Give More Value, Not More Work

More calls and content can overwhelm buyers. Find the few things that help most and make them easy to use. Ask what buyers would keep or cut. A rarely used feature may still matter when things go wrong. Check its purpose and existing promises before removing it.

Watch use as well as payment. A buyer may still pay but stop using the service and not want to renew. Offer timely help with the actual problem. Do not hide the bill or hold back promised value to make people stay.

Rewards and contact should fit the buyer and price. The source's weekly notes, seven-day homework and timed unlocks are examples. Do not turn them into promises the firm has not made.

Sources: pages 7, 12–13, 21–23 and 29–35.

## Keep the Money Test Honest

Use [lifetime-value-review.md](lifetime-value-review.md) for the churn math and [pricing-review.md](pricing-review.md) for payment terms. Count new buyers apart from departures in the starting group. Fewer exits **or** more new paying referrals can grow the customer count. Both need not improve at once.

An annual price above the old buyer's total spend can still earn less profit. You may owe twice as much work. A setup fee and monthly fee must pay for distinct value. Do not count the same work twice.

Do not copy the book's growth figures as forecasts. Nine percent churn falling to three percent gives three times the simple lifetime value, not 3.3. Its agency table shows rounded cash collected, not final profit.

Sources: pages 8–15 and 26–27. The annual-cost check applies the source's profit aim to its revenue-based pricing shortcut.

## Help People Leave Well

The book recommends cancellation calls and says they can save some buyers. Its half-saved claim is about people who take the call, not every cancellation request. Count requests, calls taken, buyers saved, later stay and the cost of helping them.

Offer a real redo or a fitting plan with the buyer's consent. State actual terms and what changes when they leave. Do not invent credits or punish complaints. Keep the exit clear and honor data rights. A sales call must not block cancellation. These are application limits; the book does suggest adding exit friction.

Sources: pages 7 and 28–29. Use current applicable rules before changing live terms; this guide does not authorize such changes.
