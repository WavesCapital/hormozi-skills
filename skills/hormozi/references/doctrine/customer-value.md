# Customer Value

Increase realized post-purchase value, retention, expansion, continuity, referrals, and safe fast-cash capacity.

## Decision checks

Use the checks that can change this decision. They are not required steps for every reply; recommend a test or implementation only when the request and uncertainty call for it.

1. Map first value, full value, retention, expansion, and gross profit by cohort.
2. Identify whether activation, delivery, churn, price, or expansion binds.
3. Apply the Crazy Eight or retention rules to the verified constraint.
4. Protect trust, capacity, and ongoing value.

For a second product or growing delivery problems, read [next-product-growth.md](next-product-growth.md). Use the buyer's need and the firm's profit and cash to choose. Team size alone is not a reason to add a product.

When promises and service fail to line up, read [team-review.md](team-review.md). Use its shared call review and service checks. For feature cuts or specialist teams, [mature-growth.md](mature-growth.md) checks buyer value beyond raw use counts.

For billing, prices or a smaller ongoing plan, read [pricing-review.md](pricing-review.md). Separate cash now from profit over time. Include take rate and honor the terms buyers agreed to.

For repeat sales, upsells, downsells or the full Crazy Eight, read [lifetime-value-review.md](lifetime-value-review.md). It covers buyer fit, costs, churn and the right group for each average.

For churn, onboarding or the customer journey, read [retention-review.md](retention-review.md). It covers early-value tests, useful service, payment choices and exit feedback without treating every example as a fixed rule.

For referrals, early wins or the actions of your best customers, read [leads-review.md](leads-review.md#earn-referrals-then-ask). It separates a good product from a good referral ask and checks the cost of each reward.

## Material Rules

For retention rewards, support speed, old-lead reactivation, low-use features or affiliate tools during onboarding, use [the audio tactics map](profit-tactics-review.md#help-buyers-stay-and-buy-again). It keeps actual usage, promises, timing and added costs with the idea.

### HZ-CV-001 — Use lifetime gross profit

Define customer lifetime value as sales less delivery costs across the customer relationship, not total revenue, cash today or net profit.

Decision logic:

- Sum recognized revenue over the relationship.
- Subtract the full cost to deliver.
- Use the result to set acquisition and expansion ceilings.

### HZ-CV-002 — Run the Crazy Eight

Increase customer value through price, delivery cost, repeat purchase frequency, quantity, quality, quantity downsell, quality downsell, and cross-sell.

Decision logic:

- Explore all eight for a full value audit; use relevant levers for a narrow request.
- Model delivery cost, buyer value, capacity and sales lost from the main offer.
- Compare take rates and profit for the same group; do not confuse visitors with paying buyers.
- Test the best-fitting option by expected return, work and risk, not margin alone.

### HZ-CV-003 — Optimize retention for total return

The goal is not minimum churn in isolation; choose price and experience that maximize durable lifetime gross profit and customer value.

Decision logic:

- Model conversion, price, churn, delivery cost, and lifetime gross profit together.
- Test rather than assume the best price.
- Protect ongoing value as price rises.
- Track product use apart from paid status. Include the cost of all service owed under a longer term.

### HZ-CV-004 — Continuity requires ongoing value

Use recurring payments only when the customer receives real ongoing value; sequence continuity after profitable acquisition and relevant expansion where possible.

Decision logic:

- Identify the recurring problem, service, access, product, or community.
- Match billing cadence to value cadence.
- Do not force one-time educational value into a recurring model.
- Agree to ongoing charges and their start date up front. Keep any access already promised.

### HZ-CV-005 — Invert the churn question

Find controllable retention levers by asking what would make every customer leave, then operationalizing the opposite behavior.

Decision logic:

- List broken promises, silence, friction, poor treatment, hidden progress, isolation, and unrealistic expectations.
- Convert each into an observable service standard.
- Measure adoption and retention by cohort.
- Treat early-value patterns as candidates to test, not proof that one action stops all churn.

### HZ-CV-006 — Value per second over seconds of value

Increase useful value density and reduce overwhelm instead of adding more content, calls, or features by default.

Decision logic:

- Measure which components produce outcomes or progress.
- Ask what buyers most value and least need. Check the purpose and promises behind low-use features before cutting them.
- Make the next valuable action obvious and easy.

### HZ-CV-007 — Fast cash starts warm and capped

A fast-cash promotion is a limited, high-value offer to current customers, past customers in good standing, or actively engaged leads—not a substitute for repairing acquisition or delivery.

For a promotion or annual prepay offer, read [fast-cash-review.md](fast-cash-review.md). It covers the full consult and checkout paths, sample-copy conflicts, cash math and delivery limits.

Decision logic:

- Choose a warm, permissioned audience.
- Create exceptional value with real scarcity, capacity, and urgency.
- Price high enough to justify unscalable delivery and cap sales.
- Exclude neglected cold lists until re-warmed.
- Match the sales window to consult or checkout capacity. Give early buyers the campaign bonuses too.
- Count extra profit and work owed, not just cash collected now. Deliver and restore useful contact before another promotion.

Limits:

- Protect existing commitments and customer trust; never manufacture capacity limits.
- Source price multiples, take rates and quarterly timing are examples. Use actual counts, deadlines, purchase consent and delivery costs.
