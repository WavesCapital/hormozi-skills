# Help a Small Team Grow

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Keep the thought deep and the words plain.

Use this when sales grow but the owner or team cannot keep up. Read the facts first. Team size alone does not tell you what to fix.

## Free the Owner to Do the Right Work

The Stage 3 guide moves the owner from doing all the work to teaching others. Find the work that piles up. Give a person a clear job. Show the steps and what good work looks like. Check their work and help them improve.

Do not hire to match a chart. Check the need, cost and time the help can free. If the team has time but lacks clear steps, more staff may not help.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, pages 3 and 8.

## Keep Good Leads Moving

Separate people who do not fit from those who fit but need more time. Set basic rules for fit. Use useful follow-up to help suitable buyers who are not ready yet.

When booking takes the owner's sales time, train someone to screen and book leads. The source table names setting and calendar help. This does not mean every firm needs a new setter.

A setter screens leads and books calls. A closer helps the buyer make the purchase. Freeing booking time may let the owner close. If sales slots still run out, check closing capacity too. Do not treat these as the same job.

In practice, agree on what makes a good handoff. Name who gets it and what they need. Pass the fit notes and the buyer's next step so the closer can pick up. Check whether good leads reach the call, not just whether someone books them. These checks apply the source to the firm's own sales path.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, page 3 image table and page 7.

## Fix What Hurts Buyers Most

The guide says to fix the top customer complaint, then move to the next. Help new buyers get started and reach a useful result. Check whether the fix works before adding more steps.

Use that focus for ongoing service work. It does not mean ignoring rare serious harm. Deal with urgent risks too. That limit is our judgment, not a risk formula from the guide.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, pages 7–9.

## Stop Saying Yes to Every Kind of Work

The Stage 4 preview shows what happens when unlike buyers pull the product in different ways. Find the buyers you can serve well. Match the product and price to their needs.

Use real results and costs to choose. The biggest sale may take far more work. A shared need can define the group; it need not be one industry. This applies the roadmap's focus rule, not a claim that its chart defines every group.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, pages 22 and 24–27.

## Count the Path to Cash

Track each step from first contact to cash:

- People reached
- Calls booked
- Calls held
- Offers made
- Sales won
- Cash received

Use the same group and time span when you compare rates. Define what each count means. More calls alone do not prove more sales or cash.

The table pairs better free value with rules that help leads screen themselves. Judge fit and sales, not just raw lead count. Add only the checks that help the firm serve the right buyers.

For speed, name the clock. An old cold list is not the same as a slow reply to a buyer. Check the wait after a buyer shows interest apart from the cold outreach backlog. This is how we apply the source's response-speed advice; it gives no fixed time limit.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, page 22 image table and pages 25–26.

## Keep the Basics Useful

Keep shared customer notes and the tools the team needs. Protect passwords; do not store them in customer notes. Keep clear roles and basic staff rules. Track profit and cash flow, not just the bank balance. Plan for bills that keep arriving as surprises.

The source names payroll, policies and insurance but does not give full legal or tax advice. Check those needs for the firm. Advice to cut unused tools is not permission to cancel them.

Its company counts describe stages. They do not prove how many firms get stuck or must buy a workshop.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 3, pages 3 and 7–10; pages 22 and 25–28.
