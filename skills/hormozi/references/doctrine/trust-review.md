# Earn Trust and Keep It

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Explain the risk in plain words, not a label about someone's character.

Use this for trust, private disclosures, promises and follow-through. It maps Alex's [How The Top 1% Actually Think About Trust](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI), published June 10, 2026. Read the relevant full span with the scoped video reader. This adds a way to think about trust; it does not replace team policy or grant access to private information.

## Name the Risk

Ask who could lose something and who or what could cause that loss. Giving someone a secret differs from giving your word or relying on advice. Someone who trusts easily is not thereby someone who keeps promises. Describe the action at issue instead of saying only “we need trust.” [00:00–03:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI&t=0s)

For a serious choice, check two things: how the person handled what they had before, and what they now gain by protecting or harming you. A good record with small secrets does not remove a new reason to reveal a large one. This is a risk to weigh, not proof that betrayal has happened. [03:09–03:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI&t=189s)

More context can help someone serve you better. It can also expose you to more harm. Share what helps the work, with care that fits the stakes. A demand for every private detail is not a test you must pass to prove trust. That last choice is our application of the tradeoff, not a command from the source. [03:45–04:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI&t=225s)

## Make It Safe to Tell You

When someone shares something hard, thank them. Do not use it to win a later fight or pass it to someone who will hurt them with it. Protecting it later matters as much as your first response. The family argument is explicitly made up, not a fact about Alex or Leila. [04:16–06:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI&t=256s)

Alex's “zero punishment” point is about protecting a vulnerable disclosure. He does not give a full workplace policy here. Our application: welcome an honest report of a missed promise, then discuss what happened and how to put it right. Do not shame someone for speaking up; do not pretend the promise stopped mattering. Keep any needed reporting duty separate from an absolute promise to keep all news secret.

## Build a Record People Can Rely On

Keep your word and give advice that helps. Name specific failures when trust breaks, not a vague verdict about the person. A short-term win can do lasting harm. His strong warning does not prove every mistake makes repair impossible. Review large risks again when they change; do not make each small chat a formal risk review. [06:09–08:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k5-57282taI&t=369s)
