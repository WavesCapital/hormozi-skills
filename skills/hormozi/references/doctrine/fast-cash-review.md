# Raise Cash Without Breaking Delivery

Read this for a fast-cash offer or a deal to pay for a year at once. The book sells extra value to people who already know the firm. It is not a cold-list shortcut or a fix for work the team cannot do.

## Fit the Offer to the Buyers and Team

Start with current buyers, past buyers in good standing and warm leads who agreed to hear from you. A stale list may need useful help before an offer. The book's six-month example is not a fixed rule.

Build value the buyer wants enough to pay for. The book suggests personal help, speed, custom work and special access. These are choices, not promises to give everyone your cell number or round-the-clock help. Price the added work and protect what current buyers already get.

Set a real limit from demand and how much work the team can do. Five to ten percent of the buyer list is a starting idea, not a quota. The book suggests ten to fifty times the old price for high-value help. That does not prove buyers will pay or the work earns a profit.

Sources: Fast Cash, physical pages 7–12 and 15–16.

## Pick the Sales Path Before the Schedule

For sales calls, open the offer from the start and spread calls across the window. For direct checkout, build interest before opening sales. Do not hold all calls until the last hour. Allow enough time for the sales team; seven days is the book's example.

Have the offer, bonuses and messages ready before launch. Add new reasons to buy as the campaign runs. Early buyers get the campaign bonuses too. Do not punish them for buying first.

Sources: pages 11–14, 17 and 21–22.

## Adapt the Sample, Not Its Assumed Facts

Changing names does not make the sample ready to send. Its plans, price cuts, sales counts and promises must be true for this firm. It uses both midnight and 7 p.m. as the deadline. Choose one real deadline and time zone for all messages.

Send sold-out or reopened-place messages only when those events happen. Page 24 says to send sold-out news only after selling out. The last email assumes a buyer backed out; that is not permission to invent one. A scheduled message still needs the facts to support it.

Keep a reply easy. A saved card or consent to send updates does not permit a new charge. Use the real agreed price, term and consent to buy. Say when an added prepaid term starts. This guide does not grant permission to send or charge.

Sources: pages 12–14 and 23–29. The original still holds these flaws; these checks guide its use.

## Check Cash and Profit Separately

Use [pricing review](pricing-review.md) and [lifetime value](lifetime-value-review.md) for the money check. Include the extra work, selling cost and any lost sales of the old offer. Earlier cash still leaves service owed. A warm list does not make all costs zero.

The ten-times-price example doubles revenue only if the premium purchase is extra. Replacing an old purchase gives a different result. The quarterly example adds eight new buyers, not the stated 22. Its assumed costs give about 3.75 times the old profit, not exactly four times. Neither example forecasts this business's results.

Sources: pages 7–8, 15–16 and 18–19. Corrected math applies the stated inputs; it does not alter the source.

Do the work, gather honest results and return to useful help before the next offer. The book suggests four offers per year. It allows two when the firm needs more time. Choose from buyer trust, work owed and actual results.
