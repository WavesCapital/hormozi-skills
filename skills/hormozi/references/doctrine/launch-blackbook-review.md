## Fix Access Without Making People Start Over

Match a repair message to what the person has done. The Zoom update says people are already registered in the new meeting. They should delete the old calendar event, look for the new invite and accept it when it arrives. Signing up again is optional.

The useful lesson is to state what is fixed and give the few steps still needed. Do not turn an optional retry into a new requirement. Do not make a paid offer the price of getting the access already promised.

## Help a Group Get Better Together

The welcome asks members to share useful tactics and try what others share. Then report what worked and credit the person who helped. This gives people a reason to keep helping.

It does not ban questions. The next page asks people to join for help and show their work. For a new group, make it easy to share the problem, what was tried and what happened. Do not copy the draft's harsh removal and refund terms as a rule for every group.

## Check the Money Behind the Hook

Several ads show how helpful next offers and repeat sales can make each customer worth more. Keep that idea. Check the actual money before using the claimed result.

Ad #5 lists sales of $100, $200 and $160, then $50 per month. It costs $120 to get a buyer and claims $240 profit on day one. The listed one-time sales minus that cost equal $340, not $240. That still is not net profit: costs to serve the buyer and other costs are not shown. Nor does a later offer prove money arrived on day one.

Check how many buyers take each offer and what it costs to serve them. Then check when cash arrives. Count monthly payments over the months buyers stay. More sales per buyer can fund more ads. It does not prove you can spend more today or get more buyers at the same cost.
