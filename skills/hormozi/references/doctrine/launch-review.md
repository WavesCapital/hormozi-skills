# Plan a Launch People Can Follow

Use this guide for launch copy, event reminders or a partner kit. The Affiliate Blackbook is a kit for one 2025 event. It is not a full guide to every partner plan.

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Make the benefit, date and next step easy to grasp.

## One Event, Clear Steps

Start with the real offer and date. Name who owns the event facts. Keep the steps clear. The signup link may not be the link to join.

The kit uses six emails from three weeks out to the live event. Use that as an example, not a fixed rule for every list. Match each message to the date and what the reader has done. “Live now” only fits when the event is live. Give people who signed up the right way to join.

The kit has a bad time-zone line on PDF page 7. It gives two different Eastern times. Use one checked date and time for a new event; do not copy that line.

## Make Partners Easy to Help

Give each partner a clear link and copy that fits the people they reach. Keep the offer the same while changing the words for a friend, team, group or ad.

Name who earns each reward and who gets it. State the action that counts and who sends the reward. A chance to win is not a promised prize. A prize for the partner is not a trip for every person they refer. Do not claim the team is near a goal without a count.

The kit shows how a partner can share earned content or bring the audience's questions to an event. Use that idea only if the real terms allow it. Explain the partner's role and benefit. Use the right notice and consent rules for the channel.

Ask why sharing helps the partner's audience. In the affiliate explainer, the winning partner can bring their audience's questions to Alex and share the answers. That gives the audience a reason to help beyond the partner's prize. This is a useful reward design, not a ban on cash or private perks.

The explainer counts an opt-in through the partner's link, not a click alone. Its partner seats and buyer preorder drawing are for different days. Page 41 has conflicting wording for the remaining-seat drawing. Keep that gap clear; do not turn it into a guaranteed seat for ten referrals.

## Reuse the Shape, Not the Claims

The kit tries several hooks for one offer. Its counts, prizes and “only live” claims belong to that event. They are not this firm's proof. Personal stories need to be true for the sender. Show real results and next steps, not made-up success.

The video pages are script plans, not watched videos. Their sample lengths and timestamps do not prove how a real clip performed.

This kit does not ban partners for an ongoing offer. It shows one use of them. Judge a new partner plan from its buyer fit, costs and results. Do not force a live launch just to use partners.

## Keep the Free Step Free

The saved launch pages show one useful path: a free signup, then a paid book with VIP extras. The buyer can say no and still attend. Do not make an optional upgrade sound like a fee needed to finish signup.

Use a real reason to buy now. This page adds first-print access and VIP extras at the same book price. Shipping costs extra. A chance to win a day with Alex is not a promised visit. Recordings named twice are not two distinct benefits by default.

Keep each claim tied to the right thing. Reviews for a book series are not reviews for its new book. A tracking time for older books does not apply to a new preorder. Show who gets what, when it ships, and what costs extra.

The signup image shows contact fields, a business-owner question and an unchecked VIP box. It does not prove which fields are required or what converts best. Use the form as an example to test, not a rule to copy. Its consent text is not blanket permission to contact anyone.

These are 2025 page captures, not current offers or proof of results. Read their images: saved text misses fields and some terms. The video frames do not prove the linked videos were watched.

## Help Buyers Use What They Get

The recap pairs a library with help choosing and using it. The workshop finds the main problem and the first tool to use. The AI offer adds help applying the ideas to each business. This is the useful lesson: more content is not the same as more help.

Start from the buyer's next problem. A bundle can remove several barriers to the same result. Do not make every buyer use all the tools in order. The recap's four groups organize its offer; they are not a fixed path for every business.

## Finish the Promise After the Sale

Show what worked, what comes next and how to get in. Keep free and paid access paths distinct. An optional next offer should not bury the step the buyer came to finish. A thank-you page is not proof of payment or shipment.

Check the buyer's state against the message, not just the file name. The saved VIP Thank You and No Thank You scripts have the same body. Both confirm signup and ask for a preorder. That does not prove two paths worked. For a new flow, confirm what the buyer has done. Then give the next useful step; do not ask them to buy the same thing again.

## Check the Promise Behind the Pitch

Pair each benefit with proof of that result. Reviews of an in-person event are not reviews of a new virtual one. Keep the original measure and time span. Revenue is not profit. Monthly prices and four-week prices use different periods.

Check the price math and its basis. A stated value is not proof of what buyers save. A payment plan, possible tax benefit or resale idea does not make a purchase free. A saved countdown is not a current deadline. Collapsed FAQ questions do not show the answers.

The full 800-book script reports survey results from a prior, different service. Alex says he did not check bank statements. It includes buyers who lost ground or stayed flat. Keep those facts with the gains. The average and median are not the same measure. One profit figure lacks its scale in the original; do not fill it in as fact.

## Know Why a Step Is Hard

Remove needless work. Keep a limit when it serves a real purpose. In the Scale Advisory page, Alex says the price and lack of sales calls help screen buyers for a group he can serve for six months. That is his reason for this offer, not proof it worked or a rule to drop calls from every sale.

An added offer can sell deeper help with what the buyer just bought. Check the team's time, buyer fit and full cost. A small first payment is not the whole price. Live access, a library and a peer group are different things; say who helps and how long that help lasts.

The full sales script says to pass if the payments put basic needs at risk. Staying with the first offer is fine. Do not let its later push to buy erase that fit limit. It spaces workshops so the team can act between them. Match the pace of teaching to the work buyers must do.
