## Check the Numbers and the Drawing

Page 119 writes $5,000 / 30 as $175 a day. It is about $166.67. The same page gives $1,000 / $25 as 40 hours; $1,000 / $15 is about 66.67 hours, not exactly66. Keep the original intact and correct the math when you use it.

Pages 217–220 use a $15 sale with $5 cost, leaving $10 gross profit. Page 217 uses 25 months; page 218 uses 10. Their lifetime gross profits are $250 and $100. Do not join the two examples.

With $30 to win the buyer and $10 collected at month 0, the table reaches payback at month 2. Page 219 adds $20 at month 0 to reach payback at once. That needs $20 of extra gross profit, not just $20 of sales with unknown costs. Three purchases at $10 gross profit give $30. A $10 cost to win the buyer gives a 3:1 ratio, not proof of profit after all costs.

Page 248 compares new referred customers with churn on the same base and time span. Do not count a referred name as a new customer. The ratio is undefined with zero churn; the net change can still be counted. Its activate, testimonial, refer and ascend steps say the order may vary.

Page 264 labels ad work in dollars. The full Leads book has a minutes-versus-dollars conflict; see its guide before setting a budget. Do not turn a short recap into a fixed spend rule.

## Give Value as You Speak

Pages 195–205 show how a line, a section and a whole piece can each hook, hold and reward attention. Give useful answers along the way, not one payoff buried at the end. Lists, steps and stories are tools, not required parts of each reply. The offer can sit within content or between pieces; drawn boxes do not set a fixed ratio.

The words should earn their place. A first-person lesson must not invent a real experience. Follow [the shared writing rule](../communication-standard.md).

## Help the Team Do the Work

Pages 259–261 apply the same methods to hiring. Ask your network, reach out cold, post to your network or promote a job. Employee referrals, community owners, in-house recruiters and recruiting firms can help run that work. Keep the method apart from the helper.

Page 262's Performance Diamond names communication, training, motivation and circumstances. Check what is missing before blaming effort. A person may not know the goal, know the steps or have what the task needs. Choose the fix from evidence. The four parts are possible causes, not proof all four fail.

Source examples of fewer owner hours, more team hours and higher firm value are illustrations, not promised gains from hiring. The closing offer on 266 is dated source context, not a current offer this skill can provide.
