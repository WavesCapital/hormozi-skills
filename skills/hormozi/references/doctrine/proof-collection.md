# Turn Customer Wins Into Useful Proof

Use this when the team needs a steady flow of proof. It supports `HZ-SAL-005` and the [proof review](proof-review.md). Keep the words plain under `HZ-SYN-004`.

Get real results. Capture them. Then use them to help the next buyer. This can reduce the need for the owner to make every ad. It does not ban the owner's face or voice.

## Start With One Useful Source

Pick the easiest source that can answer a real buyer doubt. Give it an owner and a place to use the proof. Add more only when the team can handle it.

This is not a nine-part gate. The book's contest-first order assumes you have customers and stories to draw from. With few customers, get one true win first. A small result can still help. Free work is one option, not a duty to work without a limit.

## Ask for the Full Story

Ask in the customer's own words:

1. What was hard before? What did that stop you from doing?
2. What were the numbers then?
3. What made you doubt this would help?
4. What made you try it?
5. What are the numbers now?
6. What can you do now that you could not do before?

Do not force a sad story. A buyer may have been doing well already. Keep time saved and personal change when they matter. Keep the dates and units with each number. Use a true moment as the opening line. Do not give the customer a fake story to repeat.

For a journey clip, match the same person and dates across the whole story. Keep the raw source. Do not splice several people into one made-up result.

Sources: PDF pages 10–11 and 18–20. The image on page 25 adds work hours and personal moments to the numbers. Page 14 shows an already-profitable customer.

## Close the Loop Each Week

Choose a pace the team can keep:

1. Find a real win from a source you may use.
2. Check the facts and the right to share it.
3. Save the raw-source link. Add the customer's name and date. Note the result.
4. Give it to the person who owns the ad or sales step.
5. Put the right proof beside the buyer's doubt.
6. Check whether it helps the buyer take the next step.

More files in a folder are not more sales. If the team saves proof but never uses it, fix that handoff first. Keep a list of what ran and what worked. Social likes can suggest an ad to test; they do not prove it will sell.

Sources: PDF pages 11–12 and 20–27. Exact weekdays and reward amounts are examples, not fixed rules.

## Keep It True and Worth the Cost

Ask before you record or share. Respect a no, even if an old form allowed use. Page 11 says this too. A social tag alone is not consent for an ad. Do not copy private account or loan data into marketing files.

Rewards must not require praise. First name where the proof will go. A private interview, an ad and a public review are different uses. A gift given before an ask is still a review reward if the gift depends on posting the review.

For a public review plan, use the channel's current rules. These checks were made on September5,2026:

- **Google:** Ask for real feedback with no reward. Do not ask only for praise or require a rating, set words or a staff name. Saying “honest review” does not make a gift-for-review offer allowed. [Google's review policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)
- **Yelp:** Help people find the business and give good service. Do not ask them to post a review. [Yelp's guide](https://business.yelp.com/resources/articles/how-to-get-yelp-reviews-without-asking/)
- **U.S. rule:** The FTC bars review incentives tied to a positive or negative view. A neutral incentive is not always banned by that rule, but disclosure and other duties can still apply. A platform may be stricter. Do not turn this into a ban on every paid customer interview. [FTC's review guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

The source's gift-for-review examples stay source history where they conflict with these rules. Do not copy a five-star request because the promised bonus goes to an employee rather than the reviewer. Keep the service gift free of review conditions, then use the permitted feedback path. Do not invent a legal ruling for a fact pattern the cited guidance does not settle.

Check the actual review message, not just your explanation. An ask that starts with “if you loved the service” still seeks happy reviews only. Invite real feedback without that condition. Private feedback can help fix the service, but do not use its score to decide who gets a Google review link. Keep a gift separate from the review message; saying “no pressure” does not fix an ask that still steers the rating or content. For example: “Thanks for visiting. If you'd like, please share your experience in a Google review, in your own words.” This is a new neutral example, not a quote from Alex.

Four clips from one customer are still one result. Do not claim an award is an outside check if your own firm gives it.

Plan audio and video checks before an event. Price the work before you add events or crews. Do not promise that an event or trophy pays for itself.

For contests, state the judging rules first. The book uses sales over 30 days. Different ad spend can change that count. Check cost and buyer quality before you call one ad better. Do not silently change contest rules after people enter.

These truth, cost and privacy checks qualify the tactics. They are not all direct book rules. The business's current consent and data rules still apply to calls and storage.
