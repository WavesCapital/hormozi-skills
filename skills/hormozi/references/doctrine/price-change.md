# Explain a Price Change

Use this when changing an existing buyer's price or writing the notice. Use [pricing-review.md](pricing-review.md) first if the price itself still needs work.

## Check the Change

Test the new price with new buyers before a broad rollout when that fits the business. Check who buys, who stays and what the firm earns. New buyers saying yes does not prove old buyers will stay.

Count how many buyers the firm could lose before the gain disappears. Use profit and costs, not just sales. Keep the time span clear. A churn spike followed by a drop may mean some buyers left early. It does not prove they would all have left anyway.

Avoid new lifetime promises that cost more to serve than they earn. That does not let you break old promises. Check the terms already agreed to before proposing a change.

## Write the Notice

The book uses RAISE. Use its jobs to shape the letter, not as five labels the buyer must read:

- Remind them of real value they have received.
- Address the change: give the amount, billing term and start date.
- Explain planned work that will help them, if there is any.
- State any real loyalty terms that soften the change.
- Give them a real way to ask questions or discuss hardship.

Use facts you have. Do not invent use, results or praise. Keep the new-buyer date apart from the existing-buyer date. Show each price step and when it ends.

The final template makes new investments optional. If none are planned, leave that part out. Do not promise better service or add costs just to fill a template. Explain the true reason and the value that will continue.

A temporary old rate can reward loyalty. The price gap is not automatically cash in an account. Call it a discount unless the firm has truly granted a credit. Use the firm's chosen dates; the book's three-to-six-month window is an example.

Offer personal help only when someone will provide it. Large or hard changes may call for one-to-one talks. An extension or smaller step can help, but do not promise one without authority. A needed conversation does not mean all buyers must meet in person.

## Keep Proof and Delivery Honest

The book's sample agreement rate is not this firm's proof. Its lowest-future-price line is not safe copy without a real policy behind it. Do not copy a gym's upgrades or referral rewards into another firm's notice.

The author prefers the shorter letter and credits Patrick Campbell for helping shape it. Keep the full meaning, not every word of the long gym example. A clear notice can still lose buyers; the cover is not a guarantee.
