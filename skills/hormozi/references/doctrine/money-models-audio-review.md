## What This Adds to the Shared Method

The core idea is a useful sequence of purchases that the business can deliver and fund. It is not a requirement to build four offer types. More cash up front, more buyers and more value per buyer are different levers. Multiplying assumed gains does not prove they will happen together. The opening stories add context for cash and follow-on problems; their results are not forecasts. (A02–A12.)

For money-back designs, keep **results, actions, or both** as distinct choices. State cash versus credit before purchase. The source recommends spreading a credit but also says the buyer can use it as they wish; do not turn that suggestion into a new restriction. Keep funds for repayment. A reported refund rate and a later readiness threshold are different numbers. Do not require public praise to earn a refund. (A13–A18.)

Giveaways need a wanted prize, fit, a real award and a follow-on offer the buyer can use. Entry, claim and use deadlines do different jobs. A claimed value anchor is not the retail price. More application effort can help qualification or merely lose good buyers; match it to the actual need. Do not use a historical platform commission as a current business term. (A19–A23.)

Keep the advertised basic option real even when premium fits more buyers. Equivalent discount wording does not make payment timing equal. Count all bundle items, terms and costs. Prepaid cash still owes future service; prepaid access does not prove usage. A card on file is not consent to a later charge. Customer testimonials in the interlude remain other people's stories. (A24–A33.)

Upsells should solve the next need. Optional cover has claim and support costs; a margin story is not a medical, insurance or legal instruction. Remove unneeded menu items and explain what fits. A real choice can reduce work; it must still allow no. A premium anchor must be something the business would sell and deliver, not a fake price made to scare someone. (A34–A40.)

Rollover credit does not pay off the buyer's old provider or replace a refund already owed. A gift credit with a minimum purchase is not cash. A payment plan, lender financing and layaway differ in when goods arrive and who bears collection risk. Count buyers and collected cash on the same basis. Do not confuse the share of buyers paying upfront with upfront buyers per appointment. (A41–A46.)

For trials, explain actions, fees, deadlines and renewal before agreement. A waived setup fee does not make the monthly service free. Help buyers get value; do not build the business around penalties. A smaller plan must still serve a useful need. Review-for-discount stories need the [current channel rules](proof-collection.md#keep-it-true-and-worth-the-cost), not direct reuse. (A47–A51.)

Repeat payments need repeat value. Installments for a finished workshop are not endless continuity. Bonuses need honest values. Prepay can increase today's cash while adding work owed. Four-week billing is not monthly billing. Keep cancellation easy; a paid private interview is not a paid public review or a required exit step. A waived fee expires when its agreed term is met. (A52–A59.)

Make one offer reliable before adding more. More ways to sell one useful thing need not mean many new products. Use partners when they help, with actual terms and attention to delivery and support. The closing offer list compresses earlier chapters; it does not replace their conditions. Credits, personal stories, motivation and closing links are context, not new authority or the advisor's own memories. (A60–A66, A01.)
