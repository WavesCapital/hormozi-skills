# Sales & Conversion

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Make each sales line and each note easy to say, hear, and act on.

Find leakage from engaged lead through decision, then repair availability, speed, proof, capacity, or closing in the right order.

## Workflow

1. Calculate schedule, show, close, gross-profit, delay, and capacity leakage.
2. Repair availability, speed, and proof before assuming a script problem.
3. Review objections for true upstream weaknesses.
4. Test one stage-level change and protect consent and claims.

## Material Rules

### HZ-SAL-001 — Audit the pipeline before the script

Measure schedule rate, show rate, close rate, gross profit, and capacity by handoff before blaming the closer or rewriting language.

Decision logic:

- Use stable denominators and a common time window.
- Identify the largest economic leakage, not merely the lowest percentage.
- Route the fix to nurture, proof, offer, capacity, or closing based on evidence.

### HZ-SAL-002 — Availability, speed, then personalization

Increase scheduling and show throughput by making access easy, responding quickly, and tailoring communication before adding complex nurture.

Decision logic:

- Expand useful scheduling availability and remove steps.
- Contact quickly and pull appointments forward.
- Use the lead's responsive channel and relevant context.
- Then add reminders, proof, and follow-up volume.

Citations:

- `source:100m-lead-nurture-playbook:p0044` (primary)

### HZ-SAL-003 — Five-minute contact and 72-hour appointment

Use under five minutes to first contact as the operating standard and aim to book same day, next day, or the day after.

Decision logic:

- Staff enough excess capacity to respond while intent is high.
- Offer convenient near-term times.
- Respond immediately when an unscheduled or scheduled lead re-engages.

Limits:

- Respect contact consent, local laws, channel rules, reasonable hours, and customer communication preferences.

### HZ-SAL-004 — Protect follow-up capacity

Do not fill sales calendars so completely that leads book farther out and closers stop following up on near-wins.

Decision logic:

- Track days-to-appointment and convenience alongside utilization.
- Reserve explicit follow-up blocks.
- Add capacity when booked-out calendars increase CAC or reduce show and close throughput.

### HZ-SAL-005 — Use proof hierarchy and volume

Prefer proof that is authentic, recent, relevant, and large in result; compensate for limited individual strength with honest volume rather than unsupported claims.

Decision logic:

- Capture raw customer language with permission.
- Prioritize large and recent outcomes while retaining representative small wins.
- Make proof easy to inspect across the buying journey.

Limits:

- Never present outlier proof as a typical result without the required qualification.

### HZ-SAL-006 — Match proof to the buyer

Proof persuades more when the prospect can see a credible person, starting point, use case, or constraint like their own.

Decision logic:

- Map material buyer segments.
- Collect evidence from each relevant segment.
- Place matched proof near the claim or objection it supports.

Citations:

- `source:100m-proof-checklist-playbook:p0014` (primary)

### HZ-SAL-007 — Understand the objection's logic

Handle objections by identifying the locus of decision avoidance and restoring a truthful decision, not by memorizing pressure lines.

Decision logic:

- Clarify the exact objection and desired outcome.
- Identify whether the prospect assigns the decision to circumstances, another person, or self-belief.
- Use questions and evidence to resolve the mistaken premise or accept a legitimate no.

Limits:

- Do not override consent, exploit vulnerability, misstate financing, or frame a valid budget, partner, legal, or risk boundary as irrational.

### HZ-SAL-008 — Fix upstream causes before adding closes

A close cannot sustainably repair a weak offer, poor-fit lead, absent proof, or delivery risk; diagnose those causes before escalating objection handling.

Decision logic:

- Compare objection patterns by source, offer, rep, and delivery outcome.
- Repair the upstream cause when objections cluster around a true weakness.
- Use close language only for residual decision friction.

## Output Check

Use the plain answer shape in `../answer-contract.md`. Tell the user the main problem, why it matters, what to do, what not to do yet, and the next step. Show the math and sources when they help. Keep unknown facts and safety limits clear. Do not use internal labels as headings. Run the strict writing check, fix the draft, and check it again.
