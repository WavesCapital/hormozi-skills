# Sales & Conversion

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Make each sales line and each note easy to say, hear, and act on.

Find where interested leads drop out. Choose the fix from the lost sales, costs and staff time.

For an event or bespoke-service sales review, read [service-business-review.md](service-business-review.md). Its case connects quoting, rep coaching, buyer videos, lead flow and repeat work; the ending's two-point verdict is not the whole plan.

## Workflow

1. Calculate schedule, show, close, gross-profit, delay, and capacity leakage.
2. Check access, speed and proof before assuming a script problem. Let the facts set the work order.
3. Review objections for true upstream weaknesses.
4. Test one stage-level change and protect consent and claims.

## Material Rules

For useful follow-up, a missed-call promise, price framing or splitting a sale across calls and video, use [the audio tactics map](profit-tactics-review.md#help-interested-leads-buy). Keep fresh opt-ins, old leads, cold records and consent distinct.

### HZ-SAL-001 — Audit the pipeline before the script

Measure schedule rate, show rate, close rate, gross profit, and capacity by handoff before blaming the closer or rewriting language.

Decision logic:

- Use stable denominators and a common time window.
- For booking and show-rate work, read [lead-nurture-review.md](lead-nurture-review.md). Keep cancellations in the full lead count; compare sales, profit and staff time, not just rates for the calls left.
- Identify the largest economic leakage, not merely the lowest percentage.
- Route the fix to nurture, proof, offer, capacity, or closing based on evidence.
- When the owner handles all booking or handoffs, read [small-team-growth.md](small-team-growth.md). Separate lead fit, readiness, setting and closing capacity before adding channels or staff.

### HZ-SAL-002 — Match lead nurture to the actual loss

Help interested leads book, attend and buy. Match access, speed, personal care and follow-up to the actual problem, not a fixed order.

Decision logic:

- Offer useful staffed times and remove needless steps. Add a fit check when poor-fit bookings consume sales time.
- Keep call length, start choices and free staff time distinct. More slots do not create more staff.
- Use HZ-SAL-003 for fresh interest, the time until the meeting and replies.
- Use the lead's chosen channel and real context. Match proof, reminders and further contact to their current state.
- Read [lead-nurture-review.md](lead-nurture-review.md) for gift terms, follow-up timing, accepted handoffs and booking the next useful meeting.

Limits:

- Source schedules and studies are examples, not consent to contact every lead or guarantees of added sales. Respect opt-outs and actual capacity.

### HZ-SAL-003 — Five-minute contact and 72-hour appointment

For newly engaged leads, aim to make first contact within five minutes of their interest. Aim to hold the sales call within the next three days, sooner when useful. That is the wait until the call happens, not permission to wait three days to book it.

Decision logic:

- Staff enough excess capacity to respond while intent is high.
- Offer convenient near-term times.
- Respond immediately when an unscheduled or scheduled lead re-engages.
- Separate new interest from an old cold list or ready-lead backlog. Work the backlog now; do not score it against a past five-minute deadline.

Limits:

- Respect contact consent, local laws, channel rules, reasonable hours, and customer communication preferences.
- The source prefers three days but allows five when needed. Check show rates and buyer needs rather than enforcing a fixed cutoff blindly.

### HZ-SAL-004 — Use sales gaps to help interested leads

Use time between appointments to contact new interested leads first. When none are waiting, help qualified booked leads meet sooner when it suits them.

Decision logic:

- Check fresh interest first, then contact self-booked leads to check fit and offer a sooner time.
- Check that the buyer and the right salesperson can meet. A setter talking to a lead is not proof that the closer is free.
- Keep the current appointment when moving it does not work. Give the closer the needed context.
- If the team cannot do this work, compare schedule changes and added staff against the value of the missed sales.

Limits:

- Do not invent a canceled slot or force a buyer to move. Respect consent and channel rules.
- The book uses calendar gaps for this work. Fixed follow-up blocks may be a useful local plan, but they are not a required step from these pages.

### HZ-SAL-005 — Use proof hierarchy and volume

Prefer proof that is authentic, recent, relevant, and large in result; compensate for limited individual strength with honest volume rather than unsupported claims.

Decision logic:

- Capture raw customer language with permission.
- Prioritize large and recent outcomes while retaining representative small wins.
- Make proof easy to inspect across the buying journey.
- Start with real data and write the claim it supports. Keep the population, date, and method clear.
- For proof work, read [proof-review.md](proof-review.md). Choose useful proof forms without making all thirteen a gate to selling.
- For repeatable collection, follow [proof-collection.md](proof-collection.md). Start with one useful source and close the handoff from captured proof to actual use.

Limits:

- Never present outlier proof as a typical result without the required qualification.
- Reusing one customer's result does not create more independent results. A clear comparison helps explain; it is not outcome evidence.

### HZ-SAL-006 — Match proof to the buyer

Proof persuades more when the prospect can see a credible person, starting point, use case, or constraint like their own.

Decision logic:

- Map material buyer segments.
- Collect evidence from each relevant segment.
- Place matched proof near the claim or objection it supports.

### HZ-SAL-007 — Understand the objection's logic

Find the concern behind the buyer's words. Help them make a real choice, not just say yes.

Decision logic:

- Clarify the exact objection and desired outcome.
- Use the book's three concern groups as a thinking aid, not a fixed order or proof of excuses.
- Check fit and use real facts to answer the concern. Accept a clear no.
- Read [closing-review.md](closing-review.md) for price, time, risk, trust and decision-maker concerns. Match the reply to the actual issue.

Limits:

- Do not override consent, exploit vulnerability, misstate financing, or frame a valid budget, partner, legal, or risk boundary as irrational.

### HZ-SAL-008 — Fix upstream causes before adding closes

A close cannot sustainably repair a weak offer, poor-fit lead, absent proof, or delivery risk; diagnose those causes before escalating objection handling.

Decision logic:

- Compare objection patterns by source, offer, rep, and delivery outcome.
- Repair the upstream cause when objections cluster around a true weakness.
- Use close language only for residual decision friction.
- Use [closing-review.md](closing-review.md#ask-then-learn) to find one useful change from real calls. Check what buyers hear before blaming the rep.
