# Add the Right Product, Then Make It Work

## Earn the Right to Add More

The Stage 5 guide starts with a product that works. Buyers need another thing the firm can sell them. The firm needs more profit per buyer to fund growth.

That is a reason to test a second product. Having ten staff is not. If the first product keeps failing buyers, a new one may add to the mess.

Learn what good customers need next. Test a small version with them. Check that they buy, use it and get value. Keep the first product working while you test. The guide often favors a higher price; it does not require one for every next product.

Set the test span from real job time and work volume. Explain what must pass. The guide sets no fixed month with no errors before you study a new product. Find why work fails before you blame trust or training.

## Keep Profit and Cash Apart

More sales do not always mean more profit. Subtract the cost to serve the buyer. Then check when cash comes in and when bills fall due. A hoped-for repeat sale cannot pay this week's wages.

Compare what the new product adds with its extra cost and time. Check whether it takes sales from the first product. Use measured results to change the growth budget. Do not raise ad spend just because a forecast looks good.

These checks apply the guide's call to budget and stay profitable. The guide does not give a fixed ratio for safe ad spend. Use the money sources for the math the case needs.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 5, pages 3 and 7–9.

## Match the Sale to What Buyers Get

If reps promise too much, fixing support alone will not stop refunds. Have sales and support review real calls together. Fix the claim and the service when each needs work. Give the team clear sales aids and shared answers.

The table calls for weekly call review across teams. Choose a useful pace for the firm. Get proper consent before recording or sharing calls. Keep private buyer data out of public examples.

Compare refund rates and buyer results by rep, not just refund counts. Look at the same kind of buyer over the same time span. Fewer refunds may just mean fewer sales. The Stage 6 preview warns that both can fall at once.

When selling gets less consistent, use a repeatable training plan, coach each rep on the gap in their work and give the team a useful practice rhythm. Review real calls to find the gap before adding a script or blaming the rep. Sales training and fixing delivery are related, but one does not replace the other. Stage 6's own table, page 3, names individual coaching and team cadence; page 7 gives the sales and service context.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 5, pages 3 and 7–8; page 19 image table for the Stage 6 preview. Fair comparisons and consent are our application limits.

## Fix the Work Before Adding More Work

The Stage 6 preview describes two products that slow the team down. Find the part that fails. Test a better way, teach it and check whether it sticks. Name who owns the fix and what buyer result should improve.

Group buyers by when they joined and how they use each product. Check where they fail to get value. Compare groups at the same age. A new group has had less time to leave or get results.

Fix the main cause first. Do not use “better, not more” as a ban on new sales or hiring. It means more volume will not fix work that breaks. Urgent harm still needs prompt care.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 5, pages 19 and 21–25. The same-age check and urgent-risk rule apply the teaching. The preview does not give a full method for them.

## Keep the Team Able to Deliver

Use clear shared steps and company-controlled tools. Buying laptops alone does not keep data safe. Check who has access and what happens when they leave. Do not wipe personal devices on the strength of business advice.

Promote people who can do the new job, not just the old job well. The guide calls for deliberate promotion and useful staff referrals. It does not give a full hiring or legal process. Use the right current source when that detail matters.

Source: Scaling Roadmap Stage 5, pages 3 and 7–9.

## Match Better Tools and People to the Gap

Stage 6 calls for a steady way to make ads when colder traffic costs more. Use [ad production and testing](ads-review.md) for fuller methods and feedback. It is not a command to flood a weak audience with more ads.

When the owner's network no longer supplies the skills needed, widen the talent search. Check distinct job skills rather than adding interviews with no purpose. Pay and benefits must fit the role and budget; the roadmap does not set one package for every firm.

Track what the firm owes, what buyers owe it and what its balance sheet shows. A bank balance or missing expected growth does not establish where money went or prove theft. Use actual records. Tax dates, staff benefits and access rules need the right current sources when giving that detail.
