# Help Interested Leads Take The Next Step

Use this guide for the path from fresh interest to a sales call. It draws on the 2025 Lead Nurture Playbook. The book mainly covers the first 30 days, not all cold outreach. Its four parts are access, speed, personal care and repeat contact. Choose the fix from the actual loss; the parts are not a fixed work order.

## Count The Whole Path

Follow the same group of leads through each step. Keep the dates and reasons for lost leads. A higher rate alone is not a win.

- Schedule rate: booked leads divided by interested leads.
- Show rate: leads who came divided by booked leads.
- Throughput: leads who came divided by interested leads.
- Lead-to-sale rate: buyers divided by interested leads.

Keep calls the team cancels in the record. You can show a rate for the calls left on the calendar too. Label that smaller group. Do not let it replace the full count. Check sales, profit and staff time before calling the change a win. Compare reps with similar lead quality; giving one rep the best leads changes the test.

The book's claim that 20–40% more revenue can mean two to three times the profit needs a cost check. With $100 in sales and $80 in costs, profit is $20. At $120 or $140 in sales, profit is $40 or $60 **if costs stay at $80**. If costs stay at 80% of sales instead, profit is $24 or $28. More sales may bring more costs. This math is a check on the example, not a forecast.

## Make Room For The Right Calls

Offer times when buyers can attend and staff can serve them. Show slots clearly on phones and large screens. Reuse facts the buyer already gave. Check the value of more staffed hours against their cost.

Going from five days to seven gives 40% more days to book. It does not prove 40% more profit. A one-hour call can start at 15-minute steps. That does not let one rep hold four calls at once. Keep enough room for replies and follow-up.

Remove steps that add no value. But when poor-fit calls fill the calendar, a clear price or short fit check may help. Use that check to protect useful sales time, not to block every buyer. For high-cost sales, a setter and closer may use separate calls and calendars. A setter's call is not proof that the closer took over.

## Keep Three Clocks Apart

Follow `HZ-SAL-003`: aim to contact fresh interest within five minutes. Try to hold the call within three days; the source allows five when needed. Do not wait three days to book it. Reply fast when the lead writes back too. These clocks do not start when a cold name enters a list.

Move a call sooner when the buyer wants it and the right person is free. Confirm the time and who takes the call. Use real facts in the intro; do not invent an opening or a closer's track record.

## Match The Follow-Up To The Lead

Use real needs and the channel where the lead responds. Match proof to their problem. See [proof-review.md](proof-review.md) for honest claims and [proof-collection.md](proof-collection.md) for proof the team can use. Do not invent a personal detail to make a message feel custom.

A gift sent with no strings is different from one earned by attending. State the real terms. A choice of two gifts is not a buyer's consent to pay. Gifts must be useful, real and allowed for that business.

Front-load useful contact while interest is fresh. The book gives a busy first-week call and text plan, then allows later timing to vary. Read its actual steps when building a schedule. Do not turn them into a quota for all leads. When someone replies, book or answer their question. When they opt out, stop the affected contact. Keep their chosen channel and contact hours in view.

Make automated reminders clear about being automated. Include the real date, local time and person they will meet. The book's reminders at 24, 12 and three hours before a call need to fit the time left. Do not send old reminders after booking a same-day call. Update the plan when the meeting changes.

If another call is useful, agree on its purpose and book it while both people are there. Do not leave it at “we'll follow up.” Accept a real no. The book credits Sharran Srivatsaa for this meeting-to-meeting idea.

## Make Good Follow-Up Normal

Teach the work, then help the team do it well. Notice real examples of useful contact, quick replies, care for the buyer and steady follow-through. Ask what worked so others can learn it. Give that work credit, not just an easy close.

A harder lead is not always a bad fit. Check the actual need, funds and right to decide; do not judge by looks. Use calls to find the skill to practice. A truly ineligible lead still needs a different path, not more pressure.

Look at calls booked, calls attended and sales together. Compare similar lead groups before calling one rep lazy or another skilled. The source's show-rate pay bump is a possible design choice, not a new pay plan. Praise, coaching and useful practice do not require copying its 400-call days or skipped breaks.
