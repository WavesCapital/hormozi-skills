# Demand

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Use the buyer's words. Be clear before clever in all hooks, ads, plans, and feedback.

Choose and exploit lead methods, then improve lead magnets, hooks, ads, brand association, or a real launch.

## Workflow

1. Confirm offer readiness and the actual lead constraint.
2. Choose one Core Four path from resources and permissions.
3. Compare doing more, improving the current path and adding a new one. Include the risk of relying on one source.
4. Run component-level creative tests and preserve measured learning.

When one working lead source puts the firm at risk, read [organizing-growth.md](organizing-growth.md). It explains when a second path helps and how that differs from a new firm's need to focus.

For the full Leads method, read [leads-review.md](leads-review.md). Use it for outreach, content, referrals, agencies, partner programs and the choice between more, better or new. It also checks the book's math and image-only limits.

For who to target, a free or paid first step, or whether added filters help, read [customer fit review](customer-fit-review.md). It connects customer value to acquisition and includes the no-customer starting point.

For ad writing, creative batches, buyer awareness, or ad tests, read [ads-review.md](ads-review.md). It covers the whole GOATed Ads process, its visual examples, and where its sample numbers do not act as rules.

For launch copy, event reminders or partner kits, read [launch-review.md](launch-review.md). Keep dated campaign examples distinct from rules for all partner programs.

For content production, learning from volume, personal brands or education versus entertainment, read [content-process-review.md](content-process-review.md). It preserves the August 12 live lesson's buyer, sales-path and proof limits.

### HZ-DEM-001 — Core Four demand paths

All direct lead-getting starts from warm outreach, free content, cold outreach, or paid ads: warm/cold crossed with one-to-one/one-to-many.

Decision logic:

- Classify the current method in the Core Four.
- Measure time, cash, skill, and proof constraints.
- Choose a primary method before multiplying channels.

### HZ-DEM-002 — Pick one lead method from resources

After initial warm outreach, choose a lead method from the time, cash and skills available. Build the working path, then compare the return and risk of the next move.

Decision logic:

- Start with the warm audience when one exists.
- Compare available time, cash, skill, speed, and audience access.
- The book suggests content when time exceeds cash, or cold outreach and ads when cash exceeds time. Treat that as a starting point, not a substitute for fit and permission checks.
- For early growth, focus on one method. Do more and improve it while returns justify the work.
- Before adding a method, compare its likely return for the same effort. More-better-new is a guide, not a demand to reach literal perfection.
- When one lead source creates material risk, use [organizing-growth.md](organizing-growth.md). Preserve the working path and test a second path that addresses the actual risk.

Limits:

- Regulated outreach, consent, platform terms, and brand risk can disqualify an otherwise feasible method.
- The book's rough placement-to-platform-to-method order seeks more leads for less work. Another placement on the same platform does not protect against that platform failing. That risk distinction is a cross-source application, not a quote from the book.

### HZ-DEM-003 — Lead magnet bridges to the core offer

A lead magnet completely solves one narrow problem whose completion exposes the next problem solved by the paid core offer.

Decision logic:

- Advertise the core offer directly first when the market is ready.
- Choose a narrow, valuable precursor problem when more education or trust is needed.
- Confirm that the next logical problem maps to the paid offer.

### HZ-DEM-004 — Three by four lead-magnet matrix

Generate lead-magnet candidates from three solution types—reveal a problem, sample or trial, one step of a process—crossed with software, information, service, or physical delivery.

Decision logic:

- Generate all relevant combinations.
- Score for narrow relevance, fast consumption, trust created, and bridge to the core offer.
- Test the name, image, and call to action instead of assuming the format wins.

### HZ-DEM-005 — Hook equals callout plus value

A hook earns attention by identifying the right person or pattern and implying enough value to justify continued attention.

Decision logic:

- Make the callout specific enough to select the audience and broad enough to preserve reach.
- State or show the value of consuming.
- Use verbal, visual, and audio hooks where the channel supports them.

### HZ-DEM-006 — Iterate hooks 70/20/10

Use performance evidence to allocate the next hook batch: mostly proven winners, some permutations, and a small share of new exploration.

Decision logic:

- Identify the top-performing hooks on the relevant metric.
- If the business has no past results, build and test a first batch. Use relevant examples to form ideas, not to claim the business already has winners. Then compare its own best and worst results.
- Allocate roughly 70% to top winners, 20% to variations, and 10% to new concepts.
- Recalculate after each meaningful sample.
- Keep a winner log for each channel: hook, source link, format, audience, result, and buyer quality. Record losses too. Move a new test into the proven group only after it earns that place.

Limits:

- Treat 70/20/10 as a testing heuristic, not a substitute for adequate sample size or channel-specific learning.
- The chart of 121 winning hooks shows their mix by type, not each type's conversion rate. Do not use that chart to promise results or set spend.

### HZ-DEM-007 — Assemble ads from modular parts

Produce and test hooks, substantive ad bodies, and calls to action as reusable parts rather than scripting each ad as an isolated artifact.

Decision logic:

- Develop many hooks against a small number of strong bodies.
- Match the parts to what the buyer knows. The body fulfills the hook; the next step matches the real destination.
- Size the batch to the firm's resources. Possible combinations are not proven winners, and prep-time ratios are not spend rules.
- Attach a clear, truthful next step. Show what follows when it helps; do not invent a deadline or instant result.
- Compare like hooks, bodies and audiences when testing a call to action. If several parts change, do not credit one part for the whole result.
- Track results and buyer quality, not just views or version counts. Keep winners while they work; owner boredom is not proof of fatigue.

Limits:

- Awareness levels and the five body formats are useful starting points, not rigid or exhaustive categories.
- Other firms' ads are ideas to test, not proof of their profit or permission to copy their claims.

### HZ-DEM-008 — Brand is learned association

Build brand by repeatedly pairing the business with an outcome or positive association the target market already values.

Decision logic:

- Define the association the market should recall.
- Deliver the promised outcome often enough to teach the association.
- Remove activities that dilute or contradict it.
- Separate reach from trust and buying behavior. More views from the wrong people are not the goal.
- For brand work or a change in audience, read [brand-review.md](brand-review.md). Check buyer fit, promises kept, and who the change gains or loses.

Limits:

- The source's sales table has conflicting inputs. Check the math and include brand costs; do not promise its example returns.
- A strong brand does not need deliberate controversy. Use real ties and proof, not made-up endorsements.

### HZ-DEM-009 — Launch mechanics are conditional

For a real launch, align the offer, audience, date, partner terms and delivery plan. Use event urgency only while it is true. A launch kit does not make affiliates launch-only.

Decision logic:

- Confirm the work is a launch rather than evergreen acquisition.
- Map signup, access, reminders, event, offer, follow-up and delivery. Use the right link for each step.
- Name who earns and gets each partner reward. Separate a chance to win from a promised prize.
- Match copy to the channel and real facts; do not copy old counts, personal results or reward progress.

Limits:

- Do not infer that a launch tactic should become the active business's evergreen strategy without business evidence.
- This kit does not ban evergreen affiliates or prescribe six emails for every list. Its old dates and prize terms are not current facts.
