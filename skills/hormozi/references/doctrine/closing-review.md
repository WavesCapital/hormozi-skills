# Help The Buyer Decide

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Make the point clear and easy to say out loud.

Read this for a sales call, a stalled deal or an objection. An objection is a concern that holds up the choice. Use the part that fits; do not run every buyer through every close.

For applied cases and live questions, [sales video review](sales-video-review.md) separates group sales from support, qualification from education, and early guesses from later corrected advice. It also maps the moving and roofing cases; read the case's whole plan when that is what the user asks for.

The Closing Playbook aims for a real decision. That can be yes or no. It assumes the offer fits this buyer and the team can help them get the result. Keep that frame when using its harder scripts.

## Find The Real Concern

Ask what holds them back. Listen before you choose a line. Repeat the point in your own words so they can check you got it right.

“That's a lot” may just be a reaction to the price. It is not always a no. “Let me think” could mean doubt, missing facts or a real need for time. Ask what they need to sort out.

The book groups concerns into three types: the buyer's situation, other people and self-doubt. These help you think. They are not a fixed order or proof that the buyer makes excuses.

For a buyer who wants the result but is unsure, try a useful question:

- Ask what matters most in the choice.
- Ask what would need to be true for the offer to fit.
- Ask for a score from one to ten, then ask what is missing.

The rating is not the chance of a sale. A missing feature or service is not a promise you can make. Check what the firm can do before putting it in buyer-facing copy.

## Match The Reply To The Problem

Open the source for the concern you use. These locators go straight to the needed part, so a broad search need not guess it.

**They do not see the value.** Link the price to the result they want. Show real proof from buyers like them. Ask if that result would be worth the cost. Do not fill the gap with made-up savings or a promise of success.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0030` and `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0031`.

**They cannot afford it.** Check the facts. More proof cannot create money they do not have. The book says to check the need, time, right to decide and funds first. A poor-fit lead may call for a better target, not a stronger close.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0029`.

**Another firm costs less.** Ask which they would choose at the same price and why. Let them answer. Then compare the real differences that matter to them. A higher price does not prove better work. A cheaper rival is not always a bad choice.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0034`, with page 35 for its other comparison examples. The buyer's reply shapes what you say next. Do not put every branch into one speech before they can answer.

**They ask for a deal.** Do not cut the price just because they ask. Explain the value first. Use only real, approved terms if a different offer fits. Do not invent a bonus or start date to get a yes.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0035` and `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0036`.

**They lack time.** Find the actual work the offer adds or saves. If it helps them handle a busy week, explain how. If they lack the time it needs, fix that gap or accept a poor fit.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0025`, with pages 26–28 for the different time concerns.

**Someone else must decide.** Bring that person into the choice. The book often reschedules for this reason. Support from a partner is not the same as the right to sign. Its harder partner scripts do not remove that need.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0037`, with pages 38–40 for its conditional sequence and limits below.

**They tried something before.** Ask what went wrong. Show how this offer deals with that issue, if it does. A story can explain the difference; it cannot prove the new offer will work.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0041`.

**They dislike part of the offer.** Find the exact mismatch. Explain any part that truly drives the result. Do not claim that every change ruins it. A real access need or missing feature may require a different solution.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0045`, with pages 46–47 for the method and expert-help examples.

## Use Risk And Time Honestly

Use a refund promise only when the firm offers it. State what it covers and what the buyer must do. A fee refund may still leave time, effort and other costs. It does not make the whole choice risk-free.

Help the buyer compare real options. Do not imply they can only buy now or fail forever. If they need a fact you lack, get it or name who can answer. Do not invent an answer to keep the call moving.

If payment is the only gap, use the firm's secure checkout. Do not use the book's old scripts to collect card or bank details in chat.

Source: `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0022` for best/worst; `file:100m-playbooks-100m-closing-playbook:e7c34cd48078:p0050` for guarantees. Page 23 covers payment; pages 47–49 cover missing facts and decision time.

## Ask, Then Learn

Once the concern is clear, give a truthful answer and check whether that helps. If the offer fits and they are ready, ask for the sale. After yes, move to the agreed next step without more pitching. Keep the terms clear. Accept a firm no.

Learn a few replies well. Use real calls to find what to improve. For repeat concerns, check the ads and what buyers hear before the ask. Do not assume the rep is the problem.

For a team review, use [team-review.md](team-review.md). Sales, marketing and service may see different parts of the same gap. Pick one useful fix and check its effect.
