# Team & Operations

Repair a verified people, service, handoff, communication, or cadence constraint without importing premature overhead.

## Decision checks

Use the checks that can change this decision. They are not required steps for every reply; recommend a test or implementation only when the request and uncertainty call for it.

1. Prove that people, service, handoff, or cadence is the constraint.
2. Define the observable behavior and customer or throughput outcome.
3. Install the smallest communication, cadence, or quality loop.
4. Re-sample outcomes before adding more process.

When more products or staff make work worse, read [next-product-growth.md](next-product-growth.md). It links sales promises to delivery and helps choose what to fix before adding more work.

When work gets lost across a larger firm, read [organizing-growth.md](organizing-growth.md). It covers clear owners, lead coverage, shared buyer paths, data homes and spending limits.

For service scores, call reviews, team talk, weekly planning or pay discussions, read [team-review.md](team-review.md). It gives Leila's methods and their limits. Do not attribute her examples to Alex as personal memories.

## Material Rules

For clear work assignments, behavior-based feedback, partner fit, an expensive hire or founder strain, use [the audio tactics map](profit-tactics-review.md#fix-the-work-that-holds-growth-back). It connects faster work with sound priorities and enough cash to keep the firm running.

### HZ-OPS-001 — Organization follows the constraint

Add roles, meetings, and management systems only when a verified delivery, capacity, quality, or communication constraint requires them.

Decision logic:

- Diagnose whether the constraint is actually people or process.
- Define the throughput or quality gain the new system must create.
- Avoid importing later-stage overhead into an earlier-stage business.
- For an overloaded small team, read [small-team-growth.md](small-team-growth.md). It covers trained help, lead fit, customer results and the path to cash.

### HZ-OPS-002 — High-performance communication

Design communication to increase energy, engagement, and exploration rather than defaulting to more meetings.

Decision logic:

- Choose synchronous communication only when interaction creates value.
- Make ownership, decision, and next action explicit.
- Audit cross-team communication for delay, ambiguity, and rework.
- Use Leila's three factors to find the weakest part, not to demand more chat. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for definitions and source limits.

### HZ-OPS-003 — Monday Hour One

Begin the week by reviewing prior commitments and constraints, looking ahead, and scheduling the few priorities before other work consumes capacity.

Decision logic:

- Review last week's commitments, misses, and constraints.
- Look 1–3 months ahead for work to pull forward.
- Schedule priority work blocks before reactive work.
- Keep essential service, breaks and overflow time in the plan. The sample calendar is not a rule to fill every minute.

### HZ-OPS-004 — Score service from observable behavior

Review service through Leila's five parts: concern, courtesy, full resolution, education and timeliness. Keep buyer scores separate from proof that the issue is fixed.

Decision logic:

- Sample real interactions across channels.
- Score against defined observable behaviors.
- Coach the largest recurring gap and re-sample.
- Check the whole issue and repeat contacts, not just tickets closed. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for the five service parts and survey mismatch.

### HZ-OPS-005 — Confirm role and priority understanding

Have team members reflect their role, outcomes, and priorities back; differences expose an expectation-setting failure before they become performance failures.

Decision logic:

- Ask for the role's outcomes and current priorities in the teammate's words.
- Compare with the manager's written expectations.
- Clarify the gap and create observable ownership.

### HZ-OPS-006 — Protect customer continuity across teams

When ownership crosses teams, define the handoff, status, and customer-visible promise so scaling does not fragment the experience.

Decision logic:

- Name the sending and receiving owner.
- Define the information, timing, acceptance, and escalation contract.
- Measure missed or repeated customer effort at the seam.
- Use Leila's Gametape Review when promises and delivery drift: review a real call across teams, choose one fix and track it. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for each team's questions.

### HZ-OPS-007 — Set sales pay last: OTE first, paid on cash

Decide comp after the role's skill level. Self-generating reps get pure commission; lead-fed reps get about 50 percent base and 50 percent variable. Set on-target earnings first, back into the commission rate, and pay on cash collected.

Decision logic:

- More process (brand, education, offer, script) means less rep skill and lower pay; no process needs a costly rainmaker.
- Source anchors: setter about $75K OTE, closer $120K to $150K, specialized closer $200K+. Adjust for ticket, process and replaceability.
- Commission rate = monthly variable target / monthly cash collected at target. Add volume tiers with the biggest last jump.
- Try to break the plan, review it with two top closers, then sell it to the team like an offer.
- The one test: can this pay attract the caliber of rep the role needs?

Limits:

- Do not pick a percentage first; cutting comp after a big week is the costly trap. Start conservative.
- Figures are training examples, not market rates for every role.

### HZ-OPS-008 — Recruit like you market: budget from rep value, always interviewing

Treat hiring as marketing. Set the recruiting budget from what a proven rep is worth in yearly gross profit, run at least three channels, pay team referrals mostly at month six, write the job ad as an ad, and screen high-volume roles in standing group sessions.

Decision logic:

- Compute gross profit per rep per year and ask what you would pay for an asset with that return; that is the budget per hire.
- Run at least three of: organic posts, job boards, LinkedIn outreach, sales groups, recruiting agencies, team referrals.
- Referral example: $5K at start, $45K at month six if the hire stays, so the referrer coaches them.
- Job ads: pick the platform where the audience is, choose a title that signals the right level, then hook, retain with values, and describe the work.
- Screen resumes for data-backed achievements and self-awareness; reject badmouthing and typos. Phone screen fast.
- For SDRs: script excerpt, 60-second video, weekly group role play, live feedback, then one-on-one and offer.

Limits:

- If sales capacity is not the constraint, recruiting can wait (HZ-OPS-001). Follow employment law in hiring and screening.

### HZ-OPS-009 — Give repetitive roles more rungs

Roles with many people doing the same work need frequent, earned steps: junior, title and senior, each with a comp bump, so someone can earn a step about every 12 weeks. Steps come from cumulative output plus contribution and culture.

Decision logic:

- Build tracks such as Junior SDR to SDR Manager and Junior Closer to Closer Manager.
- Tie rungs to cumulative volume (example 25, 50, 100, 200 deals).
- Use perks such as better leads or better hours where a raise is not needed.
- Hitting the number qualifies someone; it does not entitle them. Write the contribution and culture standard and who decides.

Limits:

- Not everyone should aim for manager; you cannot have as many managers as reps.

### HZ-OPS-010 — Manage up or out, and fire with a plan

Fire for missed standards, values that harm the team, repeated rule breaking, apathy or drama, after clear coaching and a performance plan so it is never a surprise. Then end it briefly, cover next steps, and remove access within an hour.

Decision logic:

- Make the gap and the training plain; use a performance plan with regular check-ins and targets.
- Plan coverage first; recruit before terminating if needed. Pick the day and time on purpose.
- Skip small talk, state the reason in one or two sentences, use past tense: 'Your employment has been terminated.'
- Do not debate. Cover pay, benefits, unused time, references and projects, and remove the person from systems within 60 minutes.

Limits:

- Severe misconduct such as theft can skip the plan. Follow employment law and get legal or HR advice for terminations.
