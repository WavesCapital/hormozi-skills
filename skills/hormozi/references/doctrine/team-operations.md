# Team & Operations

Repair a verified people, service, handoff, communication, or cadence constraint without importing premature overhead.

## Decision checks

Use the checks that can change this decision. They are not required steps for every reply; recommend a test or implementation only when the request and uncertainty call for it.

1. Prove that people, service, handoff, or cadence is the constraint.
2. Define the observable behavior and customer or throughput outcome.
3. Install the smallest communication, cadence, or quality loop.
4. Re-sample outcomes before adding more process.

When more products or staff make work worse, read [next-product-growth.md](next-product-growth.md). It links sales promises to delivery and helps choose what to fix before adding more work.

When work gets lost across a larger firm, read [organizing-growth.md](organizing-growth.md). It covers clear owners, lead coverage, shared buyer paths, data homes and spending limits.

## Material Rules

For clear work assignments, behavior-based feedback, partner fit, an expensive hire or founder strain, use [the audio tactics map](profit-tactics-review.md#fix-the-work-that-holds-growth-back). It connects faster work with sound priorities and enough cash to keep the firm running.

### HZ-OPS-001 — Organization follows the constraint

Add roles, meetings, and management systems only when a verified delivery, capacity, quality, or communication constraint requires them.

Decision logic:

- Diagnose whether the constraint is actually people or process.
- Define the throughput or quality gain the new system must create.
- Avoid importing later-stage overhead into an earlier-stage business.
- For an overloaded small team, read [small-team-growth.md](small-team-growth.md). It covers trained help, lead fit, customer results and the path to cash.

### HZ-OPS-002 — High-performance communication

Design communication to increase energy, engagement, and exploration rather than defaulting to more meetings.

Decision logic:

- Choose synchronous communication only when interaction creates value.
- Make ownership, decision, and next action explicit.
- Audit cross-team communication for delay, ambiguity, and rework.
- Use Leila's three factors to find the weakest part, not to demand more chat. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for definitions and source limits.

### HZ-OPS-003 — Monday Hour One

Begin the week by reviewing prior commitments and constraints, looking ahead, and scheduling the few priorities before other work consumes capacity.

Decision logic:

- Review last week's commitments, misses, and constraints.
- Look 1–3 months ahead for work to pull forward.
- Schedule priority work blocks before reactive work.
- Keep essential service, breaks and overflow time in the plan. The sample calendar is not a rule to fill every minute.

### HZ-OPS-004 — Score service from observable behavior

Review service through Leila's five parts: concern, courtesy, full resolution, education and timeliness. Keep buyer scores separate from proof that the issue is fixed.

Decision logic:

- Sample real interactions across channels.
- Score against defined observable behaviors.
- Coach the largest recurring gap and re-sample.
- Check the whole issue and repeat contacts, not just tickets closed. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for the five service parts and survey mismatch.

### HZ-OPS-005 — Confirm role and priority understanding

Have team members reflect their role, outcomes, and priorities back; differences expose an expectation-setting failure before they become performance failures.

Decision logic:

- Ask for the role's outcomes and current priorities in the teammate's words.
- Compare with the manager's written expectations.
- Clarify the gap and create observable ownership.

### HZ-OPS-006 — Protect customer continuity across teams

When ownership crosses teams, define the handoff, status, and customer-visible promise so scaling does not fragment the experience.

Decision logic:

- Name the sending and receiving owner.
- Define the information, timing, acceptance, and escalation contract.
- Measure missed or repeated customer effort at the seam.
- Use Leila's Gametape Review when promises and delivery drift: review a real call across teams, choose one fix and track it. Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) for each team's questions.
