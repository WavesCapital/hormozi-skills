# Build An Offer Buyers Want

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Use [the shared writing check](../communication-standard.md#final-check) on the full answer. Keep the thought exact and the words plain.

Use this guide to build or review an offer, its name, bonuses or guarantee. Read the part of the source that could change the choice. The page numbers below refer to the supplied 2021 `$100M Offers` PDF, not another edition.

## Start With The Buyer

What does the buyer want? What hurts now? Can they pay, and can the firm reach them? A clear group can share a problem without sharing an industry. Pick a useful focus for this offer; do not shrink the whole business's market by default.

Use the buyer's own words. Good copy helps them feel understood. A clever name cannot fix a problem they do not care about.

## Make The Result Worth The Price

An offer includes what buyers get, how they pay and the terms. Look for ways to make the result more wanted, more believable, faster or easier. These are levers to weigh, not four quotas to fill. The value equation is a thinking tool, not a dollar formula.

Fix the real work when it is weak. If the work is sound but buyers cannot see its value, show it better. A clear progress display can ease a wait without making the wait shorter. Proof can build trust without changing the product.

Separate the first useful win from the full result. Help buyers make progress toward their goal. Do not replace it with an easy milestone or invent a date for success.

## Design The Help, Then Cut Waste

List what could stop the buyer before, during and after use. Turn each problem into a way to help. The four value buckets prompt ideas; they do not bound the list. A missing fix does not mean no buyer can buy.

Think of several ways to deliver each fix. Group size, buyer effort, format, channel and support hours are choices. The book's high-price and low-price exercises help find new choices. They do not require round-the-clock access.

Cut things with low buyer value. Keep things with high value when the price can fund them. Costly expert help can be worth keeping. A cheap PDF can be worth cutting. Reused tools can save work, but still need care and updates.

The full design is for the team. Sales copy should make the key value clear, not list every task. Give claimed prices and values a real basis; do not count the same help twice.

## Give A Real Reason To Act

Scarcity is a limit on amount. Urgency is a limit on time. A class can have no seat cap and still have a real start date. State what is limited, or what ends, and when. Use real stock, capacity, dates and terms. A sale ending does not mean the service ends.

Bonuses should solve a buying concern or next need. Make their value easy to see. Early buyers get the campaign's bonuses too. A partner's discount or fee needs real terms. A fee pays when the agreed event happens, not from every buyer on a list.

The book prefers bonuses over price cuts in this context. That is not a ban on every discount; use the [pricing guide](pricing-review.md) for the whole tradeoff. For a warm-list promotion, also read [fast-cash-review.md](fast-cash-review.md).

## Take On The Right Risk

Find what the buyer fears. Define the result, time, fair conditions and what the firm owes if it fails. Choose a refund, credit, added service, delayed bill or pay-for-results deal that the firm can honor. These are different promises. Keep the cost of each clear.

Free work still costs time and takes space from paid work. A fixed extra term differs from working until success. A large guarantee cannot fix a poor product. Conditions should help buyers succeed, not make an owed refund hard to claim. Use true reasons for terms, even where a source example suggests making one up.

Check both sales and costs. On page 170, kept sales rise from 95 to 117. That is about 23% more kept sales, not proof of 23% more profit. Count work done for refunded buyers too. Page 171 gives the high-cost warning; page 177's no-risk wording overlooks the cost of more service.

For pay tied to results, define the measured event, pay basis and how payment gets checked. Revenue, profit and growth are not the same basis. A minimum fee still applies when results are zero. The greater of a floor or a share is not the floor plus the share.

## Name It And Test It

M-A-G-I-C suggests a reason to care, buyer group, goal, time and bundle name. Use the parts that help, in the order that sounds best. Not all five are required. Neither rhyme nor wordplay is required. Keep claims true; a name can imply a promise too.

One view does not prove an offer is worn out. When a sound offer loses response, test fresh images and copy before changing the name or season. Changes to duration, price or the product add more work. Fix a bad product when needed; do not keep changing ads to hide it.

## Check The Whole Exchange

Use the firm's own numbers. Keep sales, cash, gross profit and take-home money distinct. Gross profit means sales less the cost to deliver. Source stories and rough math do not prove the same result for this buyer. Weigh the whole offer, then give the clearest useful next move.
