# Make and Test Better Ads

Use this guide for ad ideas, weak ads, new batches, or a plan to reach more buyers. It draws on the full GOATed Ads playbook. That book adds to the paid-ad chapters in Leads; it does not replace them.

## Start With What the Buyer Knows

Cold contact does not mean the buyer knows nothing. They may know the problem and use a rival, yet know nothing about this firm.

The book offers five starting angles:

| What they know | An angle to test |
|---|---|
| They know the product and want the deal | The offer |
| They know the product but doubt its fit | Proof |
| They know the kind of help they want | A clear result |
| They know the problem | The pain and a way out |
| They do not yet see the problem | A useful reason to care |

These groups overlap. Use the buyer's real knowledge, not a rigid label. A well-known brand can lead with an offer to a broad group. Curiosity can also work with fans. Broader is not always better; seek the right buyers at a sound cost.

## Build Parts That Fit Together

Make hooks, bodies, and next steps as parts you can reuse. The body must deliver on the hook. The next step must fit both the offer and the page it leads to.

The book's example is 50 hooks × 3–5 bodies × 1–3 calls to action. That makes 150–750 possible versions, not proven winners. Use a batch the team can make and test well. Do not force every part into every other part.

Most of the work happens before filming. The book's 80/20/~0 split describes prep time for hooks, bodies, and calls to action. It is not an ad-spend rule. Little prep for the next step does not mean leaving it out. The “under 30 min” heading is not a budget for all the work.

## Find Ideas, Then Earn Proof

Start with this firm's winning ads when it has them. Use its best free content and relevant outside examples for more ideas. An ad library can help with ideas, but it does not show which ads make a profit. Views and a long run do not prove that either.

Choose bodies from demos, real customer stories, teaching, stories, or faceless ads. Mix formats when useful. These five are options, not the only forms. Use real evidence; do not copy another firm's results into this firm's promise.

Keep an ad that still works even if the owner is bored of it. New buyers may be seeing it for the first time. Test new ideas beside it and reuse what earns results. A rise in ad cost alone does not prove the market is spent or the ad is tired.

## Make the Next Step Match Reality

Say what to do, how to do it, and what the buyer gets. Add timing only when real. Explain or show what follows when it helps, especially if a form, call, or other step comes first. Do not promise an instant quote when the team must call before making one.

Show the real path where useful. The book's screenshots show a quiz and booking path, then a separate account signup. A still with a countdown does not prove an offer expires.

To learn whether a new call to action helps, keep the hook, body, and audience alike while changing that part. If several things change, judge the whole ad but do not credit one part without evidence. Check sales, buyer quality and costs as well as clicks.

## Keep the Source's Limits

The awareness charts are a model, not measured rates. The example ads' health claims, sales gains and proof numbers are not this firm's evidence. The claim that twice the leads means twice the business also needs sales, costs and delivery to hold up.

The PDF names Kaleidoscope Ads as a next step but does not teach that whole method. Its linked video captions and stills are not proof those clips were watched.
