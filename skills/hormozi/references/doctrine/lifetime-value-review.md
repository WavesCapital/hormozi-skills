# Earn More by Helping Buyers More

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Keep the math exact and the words easy.

Use this for repeat sales, upsells, downsells or a full customer-value review. Start with what the buyer needs and what the firm can deliver well.

## Count the Right Money

Lifetime value here means sales less delivery costs over the buyer's full time with the firm. It is not all sales or the firm's final profit. It still has to pay for ads and overhead.

For repeat purchases, use profit per purchase times average purchases when those amounts are stable. The book's $80 times four is **$320**, not its printed $360. Keep the source intact and show the right math.

For a steady monthly service, divide monthly gross profit per buyer by monthly churn. Churn is the share of starting buyers who leave. Five departures from 100 starting buyers is 5%, even if 50 new buyers join.

That simple estimate assumes a steady churn rate and margin. Zero observed churn does not prove buyers stay forever. Young groups have had less time to buy again or leave. Compare groups at the same age when that could change the answer.

Sources: Lifetime Value Playbook, physical pages 6–9. Steady-model and same-age checks are application limits.

## Use Eight Ways to Find a Better Offer

| Way | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Raise price | More profit from real value, allowing for lost sales. |
| Cut delivery cost | Less waste while still giving buyers what they bought. |
| Get another purchase | Useful repeat service, better retention or timely follow-up. |
| Sell a related thing | The buyer's next need, using skills and tools the firm has. |
| Sell more at once | A bigger job, more units or more visits. |
| Sell a better version | Worthwhile gains in speed, access, help or quality. |
| Sell a smaller amount | Less scope for buyers who would otherwise buy nothing. |
| Sell a simpler version | Fewer extras with a clear, useful result. |

For a full review, explore all eight. For a narrow question, use the ones that matter. Choose from expected return, buyer value, work and risk. The book's worksheet is not a command to launch eight changes.

The founder's favorite idea may not be the buyer's next need. Ask buyers and test paid demand. The book's 85/15 survey shows preference, not a guaranteed buy rate.

Sources: same file, pages 4–6, 10 and 15–27. See [next-product-growth.md](next-product-growth.md) for product and delivery fit.

## Check What the Extra Sale Adds

Multiply the share who take an extra offer by its extra gross profit. If 20% take an offer worth $100 in gross profit, it adds $20 per original buyer. It does not add $100 for everyone.

A downsell can raise profit per visitor while lowering the average per paying buyer. Keep those groups distinct. The page 21 drawing shows revenue per visitor rising from $2.50 to $3.75. It gives no delivery costs, so it does not prove a 50% profit rise.

Check whether buyers who would pay full price switch to the cheaper offer. The source uses downsells for people who would otherwise not buy the main offer. Do not invent a new qualification rule for unrelated work.

Sources: pages 17 and 21–24.

## Protect the Value and the Cash

More clients per rep can lower cost per client; it does not halve all payroll. Check workload and buyer results. A cheaper service must still fit its promise.

Annual prepay brings cash forward. It does not add a second sale on top of the same monthly work. Supplier prepay can save money, but ties up cash and adds supplier risk. Honor old terms when you design new ones.
