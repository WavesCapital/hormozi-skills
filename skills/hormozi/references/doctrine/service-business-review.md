# Service offers, sales and repeat work

Use this guide for the [service-business episode published August 22, 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU), or when bespoke quotes, weak rep output and repeat jobs may be linked. It maps a reviewed case, not a formula for every service firm. Open the relevant raw spans with the scoped video reader before applying them. Respect the task's date and video-type limits.

## See the whole plan

The event owner asks for more business. Alex first suspects poor margins, then checks sales and demand. The plan connects a faster, sound quote with stronger sales, more of the lead flow that works, and repeat events. The two points in the closing verdict do not replace that full plan. Read [01:54–04:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=114s), the [25:16–27:42 recap and channel choice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1516s), and the [32:00–34:10 focus and customer-mix discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1920s).

The original meeting, Alex's later comments and the owner's **90 days later** inserts are different evidence. A proposed change, a reported change and a measured result are not the same thing. The opening goal is **$5 million a year**, supported by the older captions and targeted audio check; the newest captions wrongly say $55. Do not turn the goal into an achieved result.

## Quote the work fast, but know its cost

- Build quotes from event size, variable delivery costs and a fair share of fixed staff costs. The $15,000 staff example is an allocation, not a verified salary or a second cost to add twice.
- Alex proposes charging **5–10 times the included cost** in this case. That leaves 80–90% of price after those costs, not net profit. It is not proof that every market will pay that price.
- Use a simple quote tool to discuss scope and price while the buyer is there. The later insert reports better live conversations; it does not give a measured close-rate gain.
- His strong line against emailing an invoice means not leaving a complex proposal unexplained. It is not a ban on written invoices, checkout or a lender's required invoice step.

Read [04:34–08:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=274s). For durable pricing and cash principles, also use [pricing review](pricing-review.md) and [money models](money-models-review.md).

## Trade a discount for something worth more

A price lock needs a clear scope. The later discussion fixes services, venue and date, with a proposed 20% headcount tolerance **if the price leaves room for it**. More work outside the deal can need a fair new price. This is not unlimited free work. [27:41–29:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1661s)

The multi-event offer trades a proposed 10% discount for a real commitment. The suggested exit term pays back the discount if the buyer leaves early. These are ideas to define fairly, not a finished contract. Early in the meeting, later events still depend on the first one going well; do not call those optional jobs signed revenue. [18:18–19:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1098s), [29:19–30:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1759s)

Prepay may earn another discount when the seller can afford it. Count the work still owed and the cash needed to deliver. Two 10% discounts mean 20% off if added, or 19% if applied one after the other. The source does not settle which. Do not silently pick one or repeat its tax shortcut as a guaranteed deduction. [08:12–09:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=492s), [30:04–30:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1804s)

## Make each good event help win the next one

At an event whose guests include likely buyers, great delivery is a live demo. Negotiate a booth and a useful short talk. Offer slides through a QR link, then ask whether the person runs events and how often. A name or opt-in is not a sale. Ask the host to send its own follow-up email, with an agreed fee if a referral books. The host keeps its list; this does not grant rights to take it. [09:23–13:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=563s)

The point of a promotion credit is a fair trade, not a random price cut. Do not repeat the apparent $90,000-to-$95,000 slip after a $5,000 credit. The next concession also differs across captions and ASR; its exact amount is unresolved. [11:47–12:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=707s)

In this case, do more of the LinkedIn work already bringing good buyers before starting Facebook. Enrichment and calls can support it within actual contact and platform rules. The episode does not prove a universal LinkedIn-first rule or allow bypassing contact limits. Founder talks may help too, within his family and time limits. [26:31–27:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1591s), [30:50–32:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1850s)

## Use the reps and help the buyer decide

The owner appears to sell far better than two full-time reps. Check their actual messages and actions. For this 90-day effort, Alex has them set meetings for the stronger closer while keeping pay for their attributed leads. Use morning plans, end-of-day checks, and weekly coaching with real practice and correction. This is not a rule that every founder must close forever. [13:53–17:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=833s)

Match a short sales video to the buyer's next choice:

- **Before a call:** answer the likely questions and ask for a useful reply, such as event size. A reply is a clue, not proof they watched it all. Offer a chance to watch on the call; do not create a needless gate.
- **For an absent decision-maker:** help the CEO or CFO judge the offer even if someone else took the call.
- **After the event:** cover billing, assets and future dates before the debrief, then discuss the next event. Those are different questions from a first sales call.

Use [19:40–22:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1180s), [22:10–25:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1330s). One call may be enough with good prep and a fast quote. Alex explicitly accepts two calls where they help at that price; remove needless waits, not useful decisions.

## Keep reports, forecasts and math apart

- The later update reports a $15,000 booth spend and a $500,000 deal **expected to close next week**. That is not booked revenue, cash or profit. [12:41–13:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=761s)
- It reports **200% more pipeline** after daily checks and the rep role change. That means three times the starting pipeline, not three times revenue. The base count and final win rate are not supplied. [17:10–17:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1030s)
- One client reportedly moves from one year to three years. That is not the earlier example of three events in one year, nor a promise to triple value for every client. [18:58–19:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1138s)
- Narrower buyer focus and better videos are reported changes. Lower acquisition cost and higher margin are partly Alex's inference, not audited numbers shown. [24:01–25:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=1441s)
- The example of 25 out of 100 events making 75% of profit is hypothetical. At the same per-event profit, 100 of those events would make **three times** the old total, not the spoken four times. Finding, winning and serving that mix still takes proof. [33:20–34:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=2000s)
- The ending's $300,000–$400,000 months are forecasts. Even annualized, those are $3.6m–$4.8m; the episode does not prove it reached the $5m target. [36:42–38:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRlSb0O5QNU&t=2202s)

The roadmap on screen is a broad prompt, not proof this firm has every listed problem. Personal family history, promotional claims, the bankruptcy anecdote and linked videos do not become rules through this guide.
