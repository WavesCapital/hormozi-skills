# Make True Claims Easy to Believe

Use this to build proof or check a claim. It also helps with reviews and demos. It expands `HZ-SAL-005` and `HZ-SAL-006`. Use `HZ-SYN-004`: keep the facts exact and the words plain.

For a public review request, gift, bonus or review-script rewrite, first read the channel rules in [proof-collection.md](proof-collection.md#keep-it-true-and-worth-the-cost). This applies to a single text message, not just a full collection plan. Honest wording alone does not make a review request allowed. Check the finished message against those rules as well as the original: remove praise conditions and prescribed review content where the channel bars them, and keep the customer and staff roles straight.

## Start With What Happened

Get the facts first. Then write the claim they support. Do not start with a bold line and hunt for facts to defend it.

For a number, keep these details with its source:

- What did you count?
- Who did you include? Who did you leave out?
- When did it happen? How did you collect it? Keep the result date, survey date and report date distinct.
- Is it a possible gain or one that took place?
- Is it one case? An average? A share of the group?

Match the claim to those facts. A review of possible savings is not proof that buyers saved that money. One big win is not the normal result. If the results are weak, improve the work and earn better proof.

Source: Proof Checklist Playbook, PDF pages 7 and 20–21. Its dated examples belong to the named businesses. Do not borrow their numbers as this business's results.

## Choose the Best Proof You Can Use

The book gives thirteen ways to make proof stronger. These are choices, not thirteen gates a firm must pass before it can sell. Match them to the claim and the buyer. Check the cost to get them.

| Proof choice | What to do | PDF page |
|---|---|---|
| In person | Let the buyer see it firsthand when that helps. | 10 |
| Live | Let a real customer tell the story during the pitch. | 10–11 |
| Raw | Keep the customer's own words and meaning. | 11 |
| Show | Show the actual change or a useful demo. | 12–13 |
| Other voices | Ask customers for honest reviews. | 13 |
| People like the buyer | Show real people the buyer can relate to. | 14 |
| Specific story | Ask what life was like before and what changed. | 15–16 |
| Size of result | Use real big wins without hiding useful small ones. | 16 |
| Freshness | Keep proof of the current offer up to date. | 16–17 |
| Amount | Make genuine customer results easy to browse. | 18–19 |
| Trusted checks | Use real outside checks that support this claim. | 19–20 |
| Numbers | Keep the dates and how you got the data. | 20–21 |
| Clear comparison | Use a familiar idea to explain a hard one. | 21–22 |

Do not score these as equal points. A fresh recorded demo can beat a live story about the wrong product. Raw footage can still mislead. A comparison can explain a claim but cannot prove it.

## Build Proof Into the Work

Pick the buyer doubt that blocks the next step. Find the best proof you have for it. Use it near that claim. If you lack proof, plan a real way to earn it.

Choose the right milestone to ask the customer. Get consent to record and share. Save the original with its date and context. Ask what changed and why that mattered. Do not feed them a made-up success story.

Give the collection step an owner. Use a pace the business can keep. Reuse a true result in useful formats, but count that person once. Check whether the proof helps buyers take the next step.

Source: PDF pages 11–13 and 16–22. For a repeatable way to gather and use proof, read [proof-collection.md](proof-collection.md). It draws on the full Marketing Machine Playbook.

## Keep These Limits

Show real evidence, not just evidence that looks real. Keep reviews honest and claims within the facts. Do not pay for a required positive rating. Any reward needs the right disclosure and must fit the platform's rules. Keep consent and private data safe.

A badge must be real and relevant. Selected five-star reviews are not a full customer survey. Five copies of one review are not five results. The book's 80% figure is a share of its selected winning ads. It is not a conversion rate or a rule to spend 80% on proof ads.

The profit drawing on PDF page 21 has no measured scale. Use it to explain lower costs, not to calculate a promised gain. The supplied PDF also lacks the one-page checklist named in its contents. Do not pretend this guide is that missing page.
