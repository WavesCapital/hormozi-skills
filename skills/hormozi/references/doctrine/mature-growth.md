# Grow Without Breaking What Works

Writing rule: `HZ-SYN-004`. Keep the ideas exact and the words plain.

Use this when a mature firm needs deeper skills or its next large growth source. Stage8 is Specialize. Stage9 is Capitalize. Staff counts describe those stages; they do not prove which fix the firm needs.

## Keep The Value Buyers Came For

Prune features that confuse buyers without helping them. Check value before use counts. A feature used once a year may still drive a large buyer's choice. Ask who needs it and what they bought before cutting it. Keep promises to current buyers.

Keep the brand's message clear across teams and products. Match leads with the right sales skill. Judge reps on leads of the same quality. A rep who gets all the best leads has an edge in raw close rates.

## Let Specialists Share One Buyer Path

Give hard work to people with the needed skill. Product-specific sales and support may help. They still need shared facts and clear handoffs when one buyer uses several products. Separate teams must not make conflicting promises.

Read [team-review.md](team-review.md) when ads, sales and service drift apart. Its real-call review helps find the break and choose one fix.

Build hiring, training and work standards that others can use. Involve the founder where their judgment or vision matters most. The Stage8 table calls for a final founder check; its prose focuses on important hires. Stage9 calls for founder-led attraction of top leaders, not founder interviews for every routine role.

Plan for critical roles being empty and for skills the next phase needs. Hire or buy tools to solve a known need. The source says enterprise software **may** be needed. It does not make a costly suite the next step for every big firm.

Give internal tech requests a clear route, scope and service expectations. Use shared hiring standards, group training and useful performance checks so specialists work together. Focused support training can be shorter because it covers a narrower job, not because needed skills are skipped. These needs do not establish a fixed hiring count, software purchase or promise to handle every request.

## Choose The Next Large Growth Opportunity

When the core product works but growth stalls, more changes to it may hurt. A new product, purchase or market may offer more room to grow. Protect the core while comparing those choices.

Check the buyer need, likely return, cash at risk and people needed. Choose how to learn more before making a larger commitment. A big possible gain is not a reason to bet all reserves. Use the firm's facts; do not invent a fixed test length or a required deal.

The roadmap describes a year-plus horizon for major product growth. That is planning context, not a promise of payback in a year. It gives no universal Stage10.

## Measure The Whole Without Double Counting

As channels overlap, compare actual total revenue with ad spend. This is blended ROAS: revenue divided by ad spend. Do not add each platform's claimed sales if they may count the same buyer.

That ratio does not show profit or prove what caused a sale. Keep delivery costs and other expenses in view. Use the available data to test each channel's impact. One blended total cannot tell you which channel to cut.

## Prepare Money And Systems For The Choice

Get reliable accounts and the right finance help before a major deal. Quality of earnings means checking how well reported earnings reflect lasting business results. It is not the same as a financial-statement audit. The roadmap names deal readiness and possible funding. It does not require a sale, loan or raise.

At Stage8, it also suggests reviewing vendor terms and using specialist finance help. Those are options tied to real cost and risk. It gives no detailed tax, legal or investment plan. Use current expert sources for those choices, not a one-page roadmap table.
