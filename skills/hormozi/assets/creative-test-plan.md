Keep parts that fit together. The body must deliver on the hook. The next step must match what the buyer will see and get. Show the real form or steps when it helps. Do not add a deadline or promise just to fill a box.

Possible ad versions are not proven wins. Choose the batch from the team's time, budget and test needs. The book's 80/20 prep split is not a rule for ad spend.

## Next Group

If this is the first batch, leave the winner groups blank. Use relevant examples to write a range of truthful hooks. Test them. Name the result that will sort this batch. Other firms' winners are ideas to test, not proof for this firm.

Keep a log for each channel:

- Hook and source link
- Format and audience
- Result and buyer quality

Keep losses as well as wins. Use that log to plan the next batch.

## Results

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| | | | | |

To test one part, keep the other parts and audience alike. If several things change, judge the whole ad; do not credit one part for the result. Compare sales, buyer fit and costs as well as clicks. Do not pick a winner from too little data or the wrong group. Keep an ad that works even if the team wants something new.
