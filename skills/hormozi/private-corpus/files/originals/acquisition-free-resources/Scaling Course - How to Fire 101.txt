How to Fire 101


This is based on the assumption you have already done all the work to ensure firing the person is the right choice. By the time you fire someone, it should not be a surprise to them. Unless a person took massive inappropriate action leading to termination, odds are it has been weeks or months coming and at this point they should know it is the result of their choices and lack of improvement.


1. Do NOT let emotion rule here. Create a plan. Pick a day and time you are going to fire this person. Doing it early in the day, early in the week, encourages the employee to get right to work on finding another job and reduces the chances that he’ll spend the weekend moping in a black hole or — worse — plotting revenge. Friday after­noons, on the other hand, often create the minimum amount of disruption to the rest of the staff.


2. Check to see what kind of coverage you will need before firing. Do you have someone internally who can cover? Or do you need to have candidates lined up prior? You may want to start recruiting and wait to terminate until you’ve got a replace­ment ready to go.


3. Tell them they are terminated. Before you utter a word, write down the most important things you plan to say and then stick to your script. Recognize what you’re up to. This is not a counseling session. It’s the announce­ment that an irrevocable decision has been made to discharge the individual.  


   1. Get right to the point, SKIP THE SMALL TALK! Start the termination meeting by saying, “Hello, John, sit down. I’ve got something I need to talk to you about.” By announcing right from the start in this tone they will pay attention.
   2. Break the bad news. State the reason for the termination in one or two short sentences and then tell the person directly that he or she has been terminated. Use the past tense. Say, “Your employment has been terminated,” not, “will be terminated.” 


For example: “As you know John, last month you missed quota for both pay periods even after excessive coaching. That being said, your employment has been terminated.




4.) Do NOT engage in conversation. If they continue to ask questions, inform them that their employment has ended and ask if they have any questions regarding next steps of their termination, rather than what has happened. Do not discuss the termination further, just what needs to be done to tie loose ends. At this point, like I stated earlier, they should have known it was coming.


5.) Cover everything essential. Be specific about what will happen next: pay, benefits, unused vacation time, references, outplacement, explanations to coworkers, ongoing projects, etc. This is one time when you can’t say, “I’ll get back to you on that.” Go through our FIRING CHECKLIST to see where they will most likely need to be removed from and make sure you cross your T’s and dot your i’s.  You should have them 100% removed within 60 minutes of firing.
